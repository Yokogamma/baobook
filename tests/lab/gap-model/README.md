# The gap model (lab)

An executable model of the gaps of the layout engine: the empty rows between blocks, kept as «stacks» (decisions G1–G8, S8 in `docs/LAYOUT-MODEL.md` §2; the specification `docs/LAYOUT-RULES.md` §1.3 and GAP is written from this model). It is not the engine: one container, blocks as card columns and rows, no handles, no SETTLE, no pushes aside. Its job is to show, by running, that the representation keeps every gap whole.

```
node tests/lab/gap-model/test.mjs [number of random sequences, 2000 by default; the other random suites scale with it]
```

- `model.mjs`: the state and the events RELINK sees column by column.
  - **The state.** The blocks, each with its canonical height `h` and the rows `e` a temporary height adds while it lasts. For every block and every column where it is an anchor, a stack: the anchor above or the top, the gap's rows with their owners, and the blocks standing in it. Each standing block has its lend, the rows its body covers by its canonical height, at most `h`. A temporary height pushes what is under its block on the screen and writes nothing. A block put lower than the gap reaches has the room above it as its own rows.
  - **FOLLOW.** It runs in both measures: displayed, which every operation works on, and canonical, which is stored.
  - **The events.** A block comes into a column, leaves it, changes its real height, or gets or ends a temporary height. Every operation is one RELINK, which judges G7 (`cap`) against the heights it ends with, after the block has left the columns it leaves: `height`, and `width` given the height the re-measure gives at the new width (a text widened and got shorter), checked at that height in the columns it comes into. FOLLOW changes no stack and never reads a lend, so a height that falls and rises again between two RELINKs leaves no trace. `fit` gives a block SETTLE put lower than its stacks hold it the rows it lacks, and never cuts a stack. `step` runs moves and width changes in one RELINK, an arrival placed at its reference: rule 1 replayed on a copy, then canonical FOLLOW. One block or several move in one step (`move`, `carry`); a block is carried to other columns (`carryTo`: away from all of them, or keeping some, in one RELINK where it leaves the others as narrowed off, M9); every column shifts at once (`shift`, refused past column 0, since it promises a settled result); an area's left side handle (`side`) shifts every child through columns left of 0 and lets its wall press them back in one RELINK, containment checked after it (the model has no P-ASIDE chain: a press into another child is refused). A dropped block is placed at its displayed row; a block that comes into a column by itself (`width`), at its canonical row. A move is made on the displayed layout; canonically the block moves the same way, at most as far.
  - **`relink(T)`, one RELINK on an explicit transition** (step 1b of `docs/LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN.md`), and every operation above that is one RELINK (`insert`, `restore`, `remove`, `height`, `width`, `step`, `carryTo`) goes through it with the declaration it makes. T holds the layout after the change (rows are targets), the steps of rule 1, the carried block, and for every block and column what it did there: left it (deleted, carried away, narrowed or pressed off), came into it (put at its displayed target: the carried block or a created one; by itself at its reference; an undo's carry back by its canonical row), or changed its place in the chain of a column it keeps (a pass: only a block of a swap, which leaves its old place freeing nothing, whatever the switches, and comes back at its displayed target; the carried block keeps its temporary rows there as rows, and the neighbour's swap step writes none of them, so the neighbour lands where the swap put it, temporary heights or not: O-21; a pass keeps its place as rows, its canonical body and the slack above it (the smaller of its canonical and displayed slack), a standing block's in place of its lend, and is made after every other departure of the RELINK, every pass measured on the same canonical rows; a block of a swap that was an anchor comes back as one (G11); every step's slacks are measured at the declared rows, each block at its start row plus the steps that moved it so far; a move cut into steps gives the rows, owners and lends one step gives; between steps, a block out of the chain of one of its columns stands at least at the row the steps give it; a block moved by hand and put at its row has under it the rows that keep the block under it at its canonical row, measured from its own canonical row, the largest its columns give, while a created block keeps the rest of the seg, so that taking it away gives every row back). The model runs it in four parts:
    - GAP.6 for every block `after` does not hold;
    - **R, the reference**: the same transition with no change of columns. Every block keeps its columns of `base` (the carried block only those it keeps; an undo's carry back none); a declared passer leaves every one of them where its order changes; then the heights of `after`, `cap`, rule 1, the passers back at their displayed targets, rule 5. A block's canonical row in R is its reference, the row at which W lets it come in by itself;
    - **W**: rule 2 as declared and the passers out of the columns they pass in, the heights and columns of `after`, `cap`, rule 1, then the blocks put at a place, top to bottom;
    - **M, where the blocks that come in by themselves are measured**: W with rule 5 made, nothing of it written, so that a block rule 5 will lower is measured where it will stand; then those blocks come in, top to bottom, each at its reference, and rule 5 and FOLLOW give the result.

    A change of order no declared passer explains, in a column both blocks keep, is refused.
  - **Switches.** `gap51`, `gap52`, `gap53`, `gap8` and `gap9` are the switches `GAP.5.1`, `GAP.5.2`, `GAP.5.3`, `GAP.8` and `GAP.9`, every GAP switch of `LAYOUT-RULES.md` §1.6 (`GAP.2` is core). `gap51` off changes only the owners of the rows a move carries (rule 1's lengths are core) and turns `gap8` off with it; `gap52` off clears the stack of a block that went, joined or promoted under the last anchor, and turns `gap9` off with it.
  - **Areas.** A block with `area: true` takes part by its extent, its frame with one free cell on every side; the model keeps extents inside and converts at its surface.
  - **Storage.** `save`/`load` keep the whole state; `stored` is what 0.2 stores, `places` what 0.1 stores.
- `test.mjs` has named cases and random sequences.
  - **Named cases**, asserting rows and owners:
    - the examples of the eleven rounds of the independent review (PR #124), rounds 5 to 11 for the engine's contract: a move and an arrival in one RELINK (`step`), the arrival at its reference; `fit` after an entry at the canonical row and in reading order, rigid steps, an undo putting a block back by its canonical cell (`restore`, `carryTo` with `canonical`), an arrival after a growth, `GAP.5.1` off, areas by their extents; G7 at a re-measured width, the carried block fitted with no vertical step, the last anchor under `GAP.5.2` off, the wall of an area's left side handle, a valid note opened as stored, and why a rules change between an action and its undo takes the inverse path; G7 judged by each RELINK (round 11), a widening that gets shorter;
    - G5–G8 and lesson 18;
    - both states of the switches.
  - **Random sequences.**
    - Inserts, growth, temporary heights, widening, narrowing and deletions, with base blocks changing height and width, a width sometimes with another height. The result is compared with the same history without the inserted blocks (the oracle of C3 and S8), twice:
      - guarded: base blocks change width only while they all stand as without the inserted blocks and never under one; then every row agrees in both measures, and stacks may differ only in the columns they touched (G4's order of arrival);
      - unguarded, as below.
      - It is exact unless a base block came into a column, or left it, while it or that column did not stand as without the inserted blocks. That block then took its place there from what the inserted blocks did, and INV-7 b is for the neighbours a block had when it came: that column's stacks may differ (the named cases «INV-7 b» show how), every other column's must agree.
      - Base blocks are not carried there: whether a gap holds a block depends on what the inserted blocks push.
      - The same history is replayed from storage in another order.
      - Carries of the inserted blocks are left out: by G5 and G6 a move by hand changes the gaps on purpose.
    - Carries and moves of several blocks there and back, some under temporary heights: the displayed rows come back exactly; the canonical ones never rise and stop changing after the first trip (INV-7 c).
    - Deletions that never move a block down, in either measure, after widths with new heights and sideways carries narrowed at an edge.
    - `GAP.5.1` off: a history of inserts, heights, temporary heights, widenings, carries and moves gives the same rows in both measures with the switch on and off. A deletion, a carry away or a narrowing takes rows by their owner, so after one the two may differ (named case «r9.3»).
    - The two measures:
      - ending every temporary height gives the canonical rows;
      - reloading what 0.2 and 0.1 store gives the same canonical layout.

- `declare.mjs` reads from `base` and `after` alone, with nothing of the model or the engine, every column a block leaves or comes into and every change of order in a column two blocks keep, and checks a declaration against them and against the reasons each action may give: only the carried block leaves as carried away or comes in where it is put, besides a created one; only an undo's carry back comes back by its canonical row; only a block of a swap, or the carried block under a declared fallback of `P-UP` off or `DRAG.B2.keep` off, changes its order (a carry inside its columns keeps every neighbour on its side, DRAG.B2); a declared pass must change the block's order with some block there; a swap is the carried block and the next block over or under it in a column they share, their order exchanged, at the rows §5.1b gives (the neighbour pinned where it stopped, after a push or not, its swap its last step: going down, it moves up by the carried block's height and the carried block stands right under it; going up, the carried block takes its top row and it moves down right under the carried block), and in a column they share the carried block is the one that passes, the neighbour counting as a block that stayed and moved. It also refuses two blocks on one cell in `after` (INV-1; an undo's carried block is placed by its canonical cell, its displayed row FOLLOW's from there, so RELINK checks it in its result) and, from the rows the first step starts from (`T.start` for the blocks a change of height reaches, which it must give, a transition without it refused; `base` for the others; a height changed elsewhere turns nothing off), steps that do not add up to a block's row in `after` or that, replayed in order, put a block on another or carry it past another without a declared pass (a block a move pushes moves with it in the same step, one step per common part), and, with nothing deleted and nothing leaving a column, a block at another row with no step that moves it.
- `gen.mjs` makes random transitions as SETTLE would declare them, from the action and never from the stacks: a carry (vertical, or sideways into free room) where every block that shared a column with the carried one keeps its side (DRAG.B2), one it reaches, even jumped past, sinking right under it with its lower neighbours following with their gaps (`sink`, P-DOWN) or going up right over it, its gap above shrinking first (P-UP), and what those then hit, the moves declared as rigid steps, one per common part (the carried block alone over the gap, then with the blocks it pushes); no order changes in a carry; a swap at the rows §5.1b gives, the neighbour pushed first or not (going down) and pinned where it stopped, the pin and the swap as steps, the carried block passing the neighbour and the neighbour passing only blocks of its own columns (swaps of blocks with temporary heights included since GEN_VERSION 6, after O-21); a carry composed with another block's width change in one RELINK, as phase 8 relinks a carry with what P-ASIDE pressed; a width change with or without a re-measure (with one, the blocks under it following by FOLLOW and `T.start` given); a block created or deleted; a height, the blocks under it following by FOLLOW (`rowsWith`) and `T.start` given; under temporary heights. Since GEN_VERSION 9 it works on extents inside, so areas come and go by their frames, and it also makes a carry sideways that presses blocks it meets from the side aside (`press` and `self`, no step), a carry away to other columns as two RELINKs (GAP.6, then the block put where it lands), and an undo placed by its canonical row: a carry back to a free old cell (`away` and `back`, the blocks under its old place rising) and a deleted block made again (`back`).
- `laws.mjs` checks the laws of a transition on them: a valid base and an admissible declaration give a valid result; a transition in columns far away, in the same RELINK, changes nothing of the others, in rows of both measures, columns, heights and stacks with owners and lends; deleting or narrowing any block afterwards, or ending the temporary heights, keeps the invariants; the blocks the steps move and the carried block land where SETTLE put them, lower only where a block that came in by itself is shown to push them on the screen (the same transition without that entry, the block keeping only the columns it kept or, keeping none, the columns it had, lands them there), directly or through the blocks it pushes; with no temporary rows, heights, deletions, departures but passes, or entries by themselves in a part, the blocks of that part no step moves keep their rows in both measures; two steps next to each other that move the same blocks, made one, give the same rows, owners and lends, and the same after a moved block is taken away; being under such a block is not enough; locality is checked both ways. The states have standing blocks: about a third of the blocks are inserted after the others are opened, into the gaps they fall into. A failure is shrunk while it still fails and its declaration stays valid, and comes with the generator's version, the switches, the blocks, the inserted ones, the temporary heights and the declaration. Each case has a history before the transition the laws judge: up to three transitions, carries away included, and temporary heights starting or ending, so that the stacks hold the lends, rooms and slacks only moves leave; the parts of a case are told apart by columns. With a second implementation (`runTwin`, the engine through its driver) every case runs on both, and the same history and transition must give the same rows, owners and lends, or both refuse it.
- `fixed-cases.mjs`: states the random histories found, shrunk to the fewest blocks, with the operation, the state the model gives and why: a width under `GAP.5.2` and `GAP.9` off where the old model measured an arrival by rows from before the change, and four presses of an area's side handle where the engine, since #133, measures an arrival against an earlier one's row after it came in instead of its reference (`engineToday`: the engine, since step 1b's second part, gives `expect` there too).
- `transition-cases.mjs`: the examples the reviews of step 1 found on the path of a pass, each with its declaration; those where a block passes outside a swap are outside the contract (`declare.mjs` says why) and stay as cases of the executor. The owner's decisions O-21 and O-22 (2026-11-09) are named cases: a swap of blocks with temporary heights lands the neighbour, and what followed it when it was pushed, where the swap put them, and they follow the carried block when its temporary rows end (going up, going down after a push, and a wide neighbour whose move up through the carried block's temporary push in a column the carried block does not come into stays its own rows, as in any move); and a block with a temporary row narrowed in the same RELINK as a move under it leaves the moved block, and the blocks under it, where they were seen when it ends (departures before moves). The laws found one more (seed 11225): a block that came in by itself pushes on the screen, through a block it pushes, a block the steps moved, which canonically lands where SETTLE put it; next to it, the landing law must fail a block a row lower than its target under a block that came in by itself and pushes nothing. The independent review of 989243c found a standing neighbour with slack in one column, which O-21 now lands right under the carried block, and a change of height with no `start`, now refused. The internal review of 62433e9 found a standing passer that grew freeing its place, a move cut into steps changing a block nothing moved, a block put under another block's temporary rows writing their push, a pass wiped with `GAP.5.2` off, and, going up, a block under a wide neighbour in a column of its own going a row higher once the temporary rows end: each is a named case. The internal review of 416e4c6 found a pass whose kept place depended on which of its columns came first, and a move cut into steps beside a departure giving other owners than one step: each is a named case, in both orders and both cuts. It also found lines of the model no random law reached; each of them now has a named case that a one-line change of it fails: a pass keeping its canonical slack where the screen shows more, a slack both measures show, and a standing block's smaller slack; a standing block of a swap coming back standing; a pass keeping its own rows above another block; a move down cut by a push putting the stack's rows before the moved block's room; a carry sideways leaving a column as a narrowing in R too, where a widened block's reference is measured; and a block put under temporary rows cutting the rows next to it, not the room of the block below. The generator's histories (step 1b's second part) found a block put by hand over a block it pushes keeping under it rows that, under its own temporary rows, pushed that block past its target: a named case. Also a plain carry past a neighbour (pushed, its order kept), the same with the hold or Shift (a swap, then the carry going on), and the declarations `declare.mjs` must refuse: a pass that changes no order, a missing pass, a pass outside a swap or by a block outside it, a wrong first block of a swap, and each reason given by a block that may not give it.

It is not run by CI by itself (only `tests/*.mjs` are); `tests/layoutgap-laws.mjs` runs `transition-cases.mjs`, `fixed-cases.mjs` and `laws.mjs` on the model and on the engine (step 1b's second part: its RELINK on the declared transition, `public/layout/gap.js` `relink(T, rules)`, checked by `public/layout/transition.js`, the engine's counterpart of `declare.mjs`, which must give the same reasons), the engine compared with the model case by case. Step 1 of `docs/LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN.md` built the engine's `public/layout/gap.js` from it, and CI runs the model against it: `tests/layoutgap-oracle.mjs` runs this `test.mjs` on the engine through the model's API (`tests/lib/gapdriver.mjs`, set as `globalThis.GAP_MODEL_FACTORY`), and `tests/layoutgap-twin.mjs` runs both implementations event by event on random histories; `tests/layoutgap-*.mjs` hold the named cases per operation. Where the two differ, it is a bug of one of them.
