# The gap model (lab)

An executable model of the gaps of the layout engine: the empty rows between blocks, kept as «stacks» (decisions G1–G8, S8 in `docs/LAYOUT-MODEL.md` §2; the specification `docs/LAYOUT-RULES.md` §1.3 and GAP is written from this model). It is not the engine: one container, blocks as card columns and rows, no handles, no SETTLE, no pushes aside. Its job is to show, by running, that the representation keeps every gap whole.

```
node tests/lab/gap-model/test.mjs [number of random sequences, 2000 by default; the other random suites scale with it]
```

- `model.mjs`: the state and the events RELINK sees column by column.
  - **The state.** The blocks, each with its canonical height `h` and the rows `e` a temporary height adds while it lasts. For every block and every column where it is an anchor, a stack: the anchor above or the top, the gap's rows with their owners, and the blocks standing in it. Each standing block has its lend, the rows its body covers by its canonical height, at most `h`. A temporary height pushes what is under its block on the screen and writes nothing. A block put lower than the gap reaches has the room above it as its own rows.
  - **FOLLOW.** It runs in both measures: displayed, which every operation works on, and canonical, which is stored.
  - **The events.** A block comes into a column, leaves it, changes its real height, or gets or ends a temporary height. Every operation is one RELINK, which judges G7 (`cap`) against the heights it ends with, after the block has left the columns it leaves: `height`, and `width` given the height the re-measure gives at the new width (a text widened and got shorter), checked at that height in the columns it comes into. FOLLOW changes no stack and never reads a lend, so a height that falls and rises again between two RELINKs leaves no trace. `fit` gives a block SETTLE put lower than its stacks hold it the rows it lacks, and never cuts a stack. `step` runs moves and width changes in one RELINK, an arrival placed at its reference: rule 1 replayed on a copy, then canonical FOLLOW. One block or several move in one step (`move`, `carry`); a block is carried to other columns (`carryTo`: away from all of them, or keeping some, in one RELINK where it leaves the others as carried away); every column shifts at once (`shift`, refused past column 0, since it promises a settled result); an area's left side handle (`side`) shifts every child through columns left of 0 and lets its wall press them back in one RELINK, containment checked after it (the model has no P-ASIDE chain: a press into another child is refused). A dropped block is placed at its displayed row; a block that comes into a column by itself (`width`), at its canonical row. A move is made on the displayed layout; canonically the block moves the same way, at most as far.
  - **`relink(T)`, one RELINK on an explicit transition** (step 1b of `docs/LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN.md`), and every operation above that is one RELINK (`insert`, `restore`, `remove`, `height`, `width`, `step`, `carryTo`) goes through it with the declaration it makes. T holds the layout after the change (rows are targets), the steps of rule 1, the carried block, and for every block and column what it did there: left it (deleted, carried away, narrowed or pressed off), came into it (put at its displayed target: the carried block or a created one; by itself at its reference; an undo's carry back by its canonical row), or changed its place in the chain of a column it keeps (a pass: only a block of a swap, which leaves its old place freeing nothing, whatever the switches, and comes back at its displayed target). The model runs it in four parts:
    - GAP.6 for every block `after` does not hold;
    - **R, the reference**: the same transition with no change of columns. Every block keeps its columns of `base` (the carried block only those it keeps; an undo's carry back none); a declared passer leaves every one of them where its order changes and comes back at its displayed target; the heights of `after`, `cap`, rule 1, rule 5. A block that comes in by itself comes in at its canonical row in R;
    - **W**: rule 2 as declared and the passers out of the columns they pass in, the heights and columns of `after`, `cap`, rule 1, then the blocks put at a place, top to bottom;
    - **M, where the blocks that come in by themselves are measured**: W with rule 5 made, nothing of it written, so that a block rule 5 will lower is measured where it will stand; then those blocks come in, top to bottom, each at its reference, and rule 5 and FOLLOW give the result.

    A change of order no declared passer explains, in a column both blocks keep, is refused.
  - **Switches.** `gap51`, `gap52`, `gap53`, `gap8` and `gap9` are the switches `GAP.5.1`, `GAP.5.2`, `GAP.5.3`, `GAP.8` and `GAP.9`, every GAP switch of `LAYOUT-RULES.md` §1.6 (`GAP.2` is core). `gap51` off changes only the owners of the rows a move carries (rule 1's lengths are core) and turns `gap8` off with it; `gap52` off clears the stack of a block that went, joined or promoted under the last anchor, and turns `gap9` off with it.
  - **Areas.** A block with `area: true` takes part by its extent, its frame with one free cell on every side; the model keeps extents inside and converts at its surface.
  - **Storage.** `save`/`load` keep the whole state; `stored` is what 0.2 stores, `places` what 0.1 stores.
- `test.mjs` has named cases and random sequences.
  - **Named cases**, asserting rows and owners:
    - the examples of the eleven rounds of the independent review (PR #124), rounds 5 to 11 for the engine's contract: a move and an arrival in one RELINK (`step`), the arrival at its reference; `fit` after an entry at the canonical row and in reading order, rigid steps, an undo putting a block back by its canonical cell (`restore`, `carryTo` with `canonical`), an arrival after a growth, `GAP.5.1` off, areas by their extents; G7 at a re-measured width, the carried block fitted with no vertical step, the last anchor under `GAP.5.2` off, the wall of an area's left side handle, a valid note opened as stored, and why a rules change between an action and its undo takes the inverse path; G7 judged by each RELINK (round 11), a widening that gets shorter;
    - G5–G8 and lesson 18;
    - both states of the switches.
  - **Random sequences.**
    - Inserts, growth, temporary heights, widening, narrowing and deletions, with base blocks changing height and width, a width sometimes with another height. The result is compared with the same history without the inserted blocks (the oracle of C3 and S8), twice:
      - guarded: base blocks change width only while they all stand as without the inserted blocks and never under one; then every row agrees in both measures, and stacks may differ only in the columns they touched (G4's order of arrival);
      - unguarded, as below.
      - It is exact unless a base block came into a column, or left it, while it or that column did not stand as without the inserted blocks. That block then took its place there from what the inserted blocks did, and INV-7 b is for the neighbours a block had when it came: that column's stacks may differ (the named cases «INV-7 b» show how), every other column's must agree.
      - Base blocks are not carried there: whether a gap holds a block depends on what the inserted blocks push.
      - The same history is replayed from storage in another order.
      - Carries of the inserted blocks are left out: by G5 and G6 a move by hand changes the gaps on purpose.
    - Carries and moves of several blocks there and back, some under temporary heights: the displayed rows come back exactly; the canonical ones never rise and stop changing after the first trip (INV-7 c).
    - Deletions that never move a block down, in either measure, after widths with new heights and sideways carries narrowed at an edge.
    - `GAP.5.1` off: a history of inserts, heights, temporary heights, widenings, carries and moves gives the same rows in both measures with the switch on and off. A deletion, a carry away or a narrowing takes rows by their owner, so after one the two may differ (named case «r9.3»).
    - The two measures:
      - ending every temporary height gives the canonical rows;
      - reloading what 0.2 and 0.1 store gives the same canonical layout.

- `declare.mjs` reads from `base` and `after` alone, with nothing of the model or the engine, every column a block leaves or comes into and every change of order in a column two blocks keep, and checks a declaration against them and against the reasons each action may give: only the carried block leaves as carried away or comes in where it is put, besides a created one; only an undo's carry back comes back by its canonical row; only a block of a swap changes its order (a carry inside its columns keeps every neighbour on its side, DRAG.B2); a declared pass must change the block's order with some block there; a swap is the carried block and the next block over or under it in a column they share, their order exchanged, at the rows §5.1b gives (the neighbour pinned where it stopped, after a push or not, its swap its last step: going down, it moves up by the carried block's height and the carried block stands right under it; going up, the carried block takes its top row and it moves down right under the carried block), and in a column they share the carried block is the one that passes, the neighbour counting as a block that stayed and moved. It also refuses two blocks on one cell in `after` (INV-1) and, where no height changes, steps that do not add up to a block's row in `after` or that, replayed in order, put a block on another or carry it past another without a declared pass (a block a move pushes moves with it in the same step, one step per common part), and, with nothing deleted and nothing leaving a column, a block at another row with no step that moves it.
- `gen.mjs` makes random transitions as SETTLE would declare them, from the action and never from the stacks: a carry (vertical, or sideways into free room) where every block that shared a column with the carried one keeps its side (DRAG.B2), one it reaches, even jumped past, sinking right under it with its lower neighbours following with their gaps (`sink`, P-DOWN) or going up right over it, its gap above shrinking first (P-UP), and what those then hit, the moves declared as rigid steps, one per common part (the carried block alone over the gap, then with the blocks it pushes); no order changes in a carry; a swap at the rows §5.1b gives, the neighbour pushed first or not (going down) and pinned where it stopped, the pin and the swap as steps, the carried block passing the neighbour and the neighbour passing only blocks of its own columns (a swap of blocks with temporary heights is left out: an open question, kept as a named case); a carry composed with another block's width change in one RELINK, as phase 8 relinks a carry with what P-ASIDE pressed; a swap with the next block under the carried one, the neighbour passing other blocks in its other columns; a width change with or without a re-measure; a block created or deleted; a height; under temporary heights.
- `laws.mjs` checks the laws of a transition on them: a valid base and an admissible declaration give a valid result; a transition in columns far away, in the same RELINK, changes nothing of the others, in rows of both measures, columns, heights and stacks with owners and lends; deleting or narrowing any block afterwards, or ending the temporary heights, keeps the invariants; the blocks the steps move and the carried block land where SETTLE put them, lower only where a block that came in by itself in one of their columns pushes them on the screen; locality is checked both ways. A failure is shrunk while it still fails and its declaration stays valid, and comes with the generator's version, the switches, the blocks, the temporary heights and the declaration.
- `fixed-cases.mjs`: states the random histories found, shrunk to the fewest blocks, with the operation, the state the model gives and why: a width under `GAP.5.2` and `GAP.9` off where the old model measured an arrival by rows from before the change, and four presses of an area's side handle where the engine, since #133, measures an arrival against an earlier one's row after it came in instead of its reference (`engineToday`, until the second part of step 1b).
- `transition-cases.mjs`: the examples the reviews of step 1 found on the path of a pass, each with its declaration; those where a block passes outside a swap are outside the contract (`declare.mjs` says why) and stay as cases of the executor. Two OPEN cases keep what the model gives today where the contract has a question: a swap of blocks with temporary heights lands the neighbour, and what followed it, a row low; and a block with a temporary row narrowed in the same RELINK as a move under it has the temporary push written by rule 1's slack, where the move first and the narrowing after it would not. Also a plain carry past a neighbour (pushed, its order kept), the same with the hold or Shift (a swap, then the carry going on), and the declarations `declare.mjs` must refuse: a pass that changes no order, a missing pass, a pass outside a swap or by a block outside it, a wrong first block of a swap, and each reason given by a block that may not give it.

It is not run by CI by itself (only `tests/*.mjs` are); `tests/layoutgap-laws.mjs` runs `transition-cases.mjs`, `fixed-cases.mjs` and `laws.mjs` on the model. Step 1 of `docs/LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN.md` built the engine's `public/layout/gap.js` from it, and CI runs the model against it: `tests/layoutgap-oracle.mjs` runs this `test.mjs` on the engine through the model's API (`tests/lib/gapdriver.mjs`, set as `globalThis.GAP_MODEL_FACTORY`), and `tests/layoutgap-twin.mjs` runs both implementations event by event on random histories; `tests/layoutgap-*.mjs` hold the named cases per operation. Where the two differ, it is a bug of one of them.
