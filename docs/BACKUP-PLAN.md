## 1. Goal

- **Sync is not a backup.** Firestore is a replica: a mistake, a merge bug or a deleted account reaches every device within seconds. A backup is a copy with a date that nobody writes over.
- **The 3-2-1 rule:** three copies (the device, the Firestore replica, the Drive copies), two media (Drive, plus the manual export on a computer as the second medium), one off-site (Drive). The iPhone is the reason it matters most: WebKit deletes a site's script-writable storage after seven days of Safari use without a visit to the site (an app on the Home Screen has its own counter, §4), and sync brings back only what the cloud still has.
- The app itself makes the copies while it is open, from one tab, only when something changed, without a server of its own and without Blaze.
- Restoring is the import that already exists: it adds and updates, never deletes.
- Without a sign-in nothing changes: the manual export stays as it is.

## 3. Practices

- **GFS (grandfather-father-son).** `restic forget --keep-last n` keeps the n most recent snapshots; `--keep-daily n` keeps, «for the last n days which have one or more snapshots, only the most recent one for each day»; weekly and monthly likewise; «a snapshot only needs to match a single option to be kept (the results are ORed)». Time Machine «automatically makes hourly backups for the past 24 hours, daily backups for the past month, and weekly backups for all previous months», and deletes the oldest when the disk is full. §6.2 uses the restic semantics.
- **RPO and RTO.** The recovery point is the age of the newest copy: at most an hour (three on a phone) of **foreground** use while some device has the app open and in front (timers are throttled in hidden tabs and frozen on a backgrounded phone, so a phone in a pocket makes no copies), plus however long every device was closed; the manual export covers the rest. The recovery time is minutes: open the list, pick a copy, import; no server is involved, and a copy is readable without the app (§6.8).
- **How others do it.** Obsidian's File Recovery keeps local snapshots at least 5 minutes apart for 7 days, per device, outside the vault. Joplin saves a revision every 10 minutes, keeps them 90 days by default and syncs them with the notes. Standard Notes writes an encrypted backup every day to a folder on the computer and can e-mail a daily encrypted backup. Notion keeps page history 7, 30 or 90 days by plan, the trash 30 days, exports the workspace by hand, and restores from its own database snapshots of the last 30 days on request. Bear backs up by hand into one `.bear2bk` archive (a zip of text bundles) and its restore replaces every note; its iCloud sync is not a backup. The lessons taken: copies separate from sync, several generations, a copy that opens without the app, a restore that adds instead of replacing.

## 4. Facts about Google Drive and the browser that shape the design

- **Scope.** `drive.file` is **non-sensitive**: it lets the app «create new Drive files, or modify existing files, that you open with an app», and `files.list` returns only those. «If your app utilizes only non-sensitive scopes, it is not mandatory for your app to complete the app verification process»; a lighter «brand verification» is only for showing the name and logo. The consent screen is already in production. `openid` and `email` are non-sensitive too.
- **Tokens.** «User access tokens automatically expire after one hour». In the browser Google issues no refresh token: the Google Identity Services token model renews a token only by `requestAccessToken()` «from a user-driven event such as a button press», and Firebase Auth hands out the Google access token only inside the result of a sign-in, `reauthenticateWithPopup`/`reauthenticateWithRedirect` or a link (`GoogleAuthProvider.credentialFromResult(result).accessToken`, after `provider.addScope(…)`); Firebase renews its own ID token, not Google's access token. A refresh token exists only for a server: the authorization code flow with `access_type=offline`, returned on the first exchange only (`prompt=consent` forces a new one, and the old one stays valid), renewed at `https://oauth2.googleapis.com/token` with `grant_type=refresh_token`. It stops working when revoked or unused for six months; 100 live refresh tokens per account per client. **Revocation is per project, not per token:** at `https://oauth2.googleapis.com/revoke` «revocation removes all OAuth 2.0 scopes previously granted to a project, invalidating any issued access or refresh tokens for all clients registered under that project». The 7-day expiry of the «Testing» publishing status does not apply: the screen is «In production». With `openid` in the scope the token endpoint also returns an `id_token` naming the Google account (`sub`, `email`); a Firebase ID token names the same account under `firebase.identities["google.com"][0]` and its `email` claim. The token endpoint's answer also lists the scopes the user actually granted (`scope`); Google's guide says to check them, since a user may grant only some of the requested ones.
- **Uploads.** Simple and multipart uploads take «5 MB or less»; «for large files (greater than 5 MB)» the resumable protocol: a `POST` with the metadata and `X-Upload-Content-Type`/`X-Upload-Content-Length`, the session URI in the `Location` header, then `PUT` chunks «in multiples of 256 KB», a session valid for a week; the file limit is 5 TB. **CORS probe (2026-10-18, without a token):** the preflight of `POST /upload/drive/v3/files?uploadType=resumable` from `https://baobook.matamata.app` allows `POST, PUT, PATCH, DELETE` and the headers `authorization, content-type, x-upload-content-type, x-upload-content-length`; the 401 answer exposes `X-GUploader-UploadID`, not `Location`. Whether `Location` is exposed on a 200 is what B1 checks; if it is not, the session URI is rebuilt as `…?uploadType=resumable&upload_id={X-GUploader-UploadID}`, and if that fails too, the Worker proxies the upload (§6.5).
- **Revisions.** Of a non-Google file «any blob file revision, other than the head revision, that's not designated as "Keep Forever" is purgeable», kept about 30 days and «purged earlier if a file has 100 revisions»; «up to 200 revisions can be set to "Keep Forever"» and they «count towards your storage limit». Drive Help: «a version might be permanently deleted after 30 days or if there are 100 newer versions». So a copy is a file of its own, and a copy «for today» is a new file plus the deletion of the earlier one, never `files.update` with new content.
- **Deleting.** `files.delete` «permanently delete[s] a Drive file without moving it to the trash»; trashed files «are automatically deleted after 30 days» and count toward storage until then.
- **Properties.** `appProperties` are private to the app, at most 30 per app per file, «124 bytes per property string (including both key and value)», and searchable: `appProperties has { key='…' and value='…' }`. `files.list` orders by `createdTime desc` on request and pages with `nextPageToken`; `createdTime` is Drive's server time, and so is the `Date` header of every answer, which Google exposes to CORS on the files and upload endpoints (`Access-Control-Expose-Headers`, probed 2026-10-18; `Date` is not safelisted by itself).
- **Quotas.** Limits are in quota units: `files.list` 100, `files.get` 5, a download 200, `files.update` 50, other calls 5 (no figure is given for `files.create` or `files.delete`; §6.10 assumes 50); 1 000 000 units per minute per project and 325 000 per minute per user; 400 000 000 units a day before charges. Storage: 15 GB shared with Gmail and Photos; the trash counts.
- **Browser.** `CompressionStream`/`DecompressionStream` with `gzip` are Baseline, «available across browsers since May 2023» (Chromium 80, Firefox 113, Safari 16.4); a gzip file starts with the bytes `1f 8b`. `showDirectoryPicker` is Chromium only (Chrome and Edge 86+), secure context, user activation; since Chrome 122 a granted folder permission can persist, and installed apps persist it by default. WebKit: a 7-day cap on script-writable storage in Safari; «web applications added to the home screen are not part of Safari and thus have their own counter».
- **Cloudflare.** Secrets are encrypted bindings, «not visible within Wrangler or Cloudflare dashboard after you define them», up to 5 KB each. Workers Free: 100 000 requests a day, 10 ms CPU per request, 100 MB request body, 50 subrequests. KV Free: 100 000 reads and 1 000 writes a day, 1 GB; KV is eventually consistent: «changes may take up to 60 seconds or more to be visible in other global network locations», and «negative lookups indicating that the key does not exist are also cached»; it has no atomic compare-and-swap, so two writers of one key race, and one key takes at most one write a second. The same Worker serves `/__/auth/*` (SYNC-PLAN §4.12), so its daily request budget is shared with sign-in.
- **Firebase.** An ID token is verified with Google's public keys (`kid`, RS256; the x509 list and the JWK list at `https://www.googleapis.com/service_accounts/v1/jwk/securetoken@system.gserviceaccount.com`, cached by `Cache-Control: max-age`, about 6 hours), `aud` = `baobook-3fe7f`, `iss` = `https://securetoken.google.com/baobook-3fe7f`, `exp`, `iat`, `sub` = uid. Firestore Spark: 50 000 reads, 20 000 writes, 20 000 deletes a day; this stage adds no Firestore traffic. Since 2025 the console shows an OAuth client secret only once, when it is created; a client may hold two secrets at a time («Add secret»; «both secrets can be used until you manually disable them»), which is how a second secret is made for a client whose first one is masked.

## 6. Design

### 6.1 What is built

- `public/backup.js`: a pure core like `sync.js` and `cloud.js` (UMD, no build step, no DOM, no UI strings): the payload builder, the hash, `prune()`, file names and properties, the Drive client over an injected `fetch` with its base URL. `index.html` wires it to IndexedDB, the sync status, the gear menu and the leader tab. Like the other two scripts it is loaded with `?b=BUILD` and an `integrity` hash, listed in `FILES` of `tests/hashes.mjs`, and every change of `public/` bumps `BUILD` (2026-10-24).
- The `sync` store gets the keys `backup:last` {at, checked, retryAt, hash, notes, blocks, versions, fileId, name} (`at`: when a copy was last confirmed on Drive, by upload or by listing; `checked`: the last check; `retryAt`: the next try after a failure; all three by the device clock; the skew between Drive's clock and the device's is never stored, §6.5), `backup:folder` {id, site, readme, tz} and `backup:state` {mode: `off` | `tap` | `broker`, since, nonce} (`nonce`: the connect attempt this device started, §6.6); they belong to (env, uid), and B1 adds the prefix `backup:` to the list SYNC-PLAN §4.11 clears on a change of owner. Every write of a `backup:*` key goes through one IndexedDB transaction with the context check of §6.5, as the engine's `tx()` does. No new store, no change of `format` or of the IndexedDB version (2026-10-24). Tokens live in memory only.
- The gear menu gets a row «Резервні копії» with the state (§6.9) and a panel for the list and the restore (§6.8). The «Імпорт» button accepts a gzip file as well, by its first bytes, whatever its name.
- `worker/index.js` today proxies `/__/auth/*`, answers 404 for every other `/__/` path and hands everything else to the `ASSETS` binding (SYNC-PLAN §4.12; `tests/worker.mjs` tests it in Node with `fetch` replaced) (2026-10-24); the dispatch of `/api/drive/*` keeps that 404; B2 adds four routes under `/api/drive/`, a KV namespace and two secrets (§6.6); `assets.run_worker_first` grows to `["/__/*", "/api/*"]`; CI `paths` already cover `worker/**` and `wrangler.jsonc`.
- The service worker (one cache set per build, SYNC-PLAN §4.13) leaves `/api/` alone as it leaves `/__/` (2026-10-24): a navigation to the callback must never be answered with the shell page after the worker's five-second wait, and no broker answer is ever cached; `./backup.js?b=BUILD` joins `PRECACHE`, its hash read from the page like the other files, so the offline start has the core.
- B1 has no allowlist of its own: the gate «Синхронізовано» (§6.3) is unreachable for an account the rules refuse, so only the owner's account ever writes a copy; B2's broker checks the uid explicitly.

### 6.2 Kinds of copies, retention and safety nets

Every copy is a file. The «copy for today» is not overwritten in place: a new file is uploaded and the pruner deletes the earlier copies of the same backup day, except what the 3-newest rule keeps (so today may hold up to three files). The **backup day** of a copy is its calendar date in the account's zone, moved one day back when the hour is under 4 (not «minus four hours, then the date»: that would move 04:30 of a DST-change day into the day before), so the last copy before 04:00 is the one that stays for its day. Weeks are ISO weeks and months calendar months of the backup day. Time is Drive's `createdTime`, server time, never the writer's clock (§6.4); files are ordered by (`createdTime`, `id`) after the whole listing is loaded, so two copies with one timestamp, or a listing that came back in another order, give every pruner the same ranking. **The zone belongs to the set of copies, not to the device:** the device that creates the folder writes its IANA zone into the folder's `appProperties.tz`, every device reads `policy.tz` from there, and «local time» in §2 means that zone (§9). A device in another zone sees the boundary at another wall-clock hour but computes the same days, weeks and months, so every pruner keeps the same generations; with a zone per device a copy made at 03:00 UTC on March 1 would be February's last copy for one device and a March copy for another, and the second could delete the only copy of February. The date is computed with `Intl.DateTimeFormat` in `policy.tz`, so the function is pure and a test may run it in `Europe/Kyiv`, in UTC and across both DST nights. `prune(files, now, policy, uncertain)` works over the listing of the folder; `policy = {last: 3, daily: 7, weekly: 4, monthly: 12, dayStart: 4, tz, maxRemove: 3}`; `uncertain` is set when `now` is the device's own clock with no skew measured in this run (§6.5 step 5), and then no guarded file is released, whatever its `keepUntil`; a file with `keepPending` (a guard set under uncertain time, below) is kept by every pruner until a run with a measured skew gives it a `keepUntil`:

```
sorted = files ordered by (createdTime, id) descending
guards  = { f : f.guard }                          // guard copies (below) live by their term alone
regular = sorted ∖ guards                          // the copies that take part in the rotation
keep    = the first policy.last of regular         // the 3 newest, whatever their age
     ∪ { f : f.keepUntil > now ∨ (uncertain ∧ f.keepUntil) ∨ f.keepPending }   // the shrink guard (below); a guard never expires on an untrusted clock
for (key, n) in [(day, 7), (week, 4), (month, 12)]:
  seen = ∅
  for f in regular:                                // newest first: the first file of a key is the newest of that day, week or month
    if key(f) ∈ seen: continue
    seen ∪= key(f); keep ∪= f
    if |seen| = n: break
remove = the oldest policy.maxRemove of (sorted ∖ keep)   // the rest goes with later copies
assert |regular ∖ remove| ≥ min(policy.last, |regular|)    // by construction, also for the first one or two copies; guards go only by their term
```

- Restic semantics: the «last 7 days» are the last seven days that have a copy, so a pause in use never eats the generations; the sets are ORed, and a file that is both a daily and a weekly is kept once. A deletion is retried with the next copy, and a 404 on `files.delete` counts as done.
- Only files of this site with `appProperties.app = baobook` and `kind = backup` are listed and only they can be removed; the folder itself, a file the app does not recognise and a file whose metadata it cannot read are never touched. Removal is `files.delete`, permanent, because a trashed copy still counts toward storage for 30 days and hourly copies would pile up there; the generations and the shrink guard are the protection, not the trash.
- **The shrink guard.** Before uploading, three counts of the new payload are compared with the `appProperties` of the newest listed regular copy: the notes of the synced set (§6.4: the trash and the archive included, an empty note without a cloud base left out), blocks over those notes, and **named** versions that are not local-only (conflict copies and local-only versions differ by device, §6.4, and are not counted). One constant, `SHRINK = {notes: {pct: 10, min: 3}, blocks: {pct: 20, min: 20}, versions: {pct: 20, min: 2}}`, each axis with a test: a fall by at least the percentage and at least the minimum on any axis makes a **guard copy** of the newest listed regular copy (the previous one; guard copies are never the reference) with `files.copy` **before** the new copy is uploaded (§6.5 step 6): a new file in the same folder, named `…-guard.json.gz`, whose `appProperties` are set in the same request: `guard = 1`, `reason`, the `hash`, `bytes` and counts of the original, and `keepUntil = nowDrive + 30 days`; under uncertain time (no skew measured in this run) `keepPending = 1` and no `keepUntil`, because a term computed by an unverified clock could already be over for a device with the right time. The guard is a new file, so it is in nobody's earlier listing and in no remove set computed before it existed; protecting the original in place with a metadata update would race with another device that read its listing a moment earlier and is about to delete that very file. A guard copy takes no part in the 3-newest and generation ranking: it lives by its term alone and goes when the term is over. The first run with a measured skew, on any device, that lists a `keepPending` file converts it to `keepUntil = nowDrive + 30 days` and clears the flag (one metadata update; nobody deletes a pending file, so it races with nothing), so the 30 days count from the first trusted time. When the copy fails, nothing is uploaded and the attempt is retried (§6.3), so a shrunk copy never sits on Drive without its guard, and the next check cannot stop at an equal hash with the guard missing. The new copy follows and the status says so (§6.9). The pruner keeps a file whose `keepUntil` is in the future. A loss that stays under every threshold copy after copy is the risk the generations cover, not the guard.
- Expected shelf: 7 + 4 + 12 generations with overlaps, plus up to 3 newest and the guarded ones: about 21–24 files. A copy is a few MB today (§6.4); the plan budgets 10 MB a file, about 210–240 MB.

### 6.3 When a copy is made and how often

A **check** is one run of the leader tab's one-minute timer, also made on `visibilitychange` to visible, on `online`, and when the sync status becomes «Синхронізовано»; with a signed-in account it first probes the broker when `mode ≠ broker` (§6.6: at most once in 10 minutes, and once a minute for two minutes after a connect attempt), then asks whether a copy is **due**: the account is signed in and `backup:state.mode ≠ off`; the device is online; the sync status is «Синхронізовано» (SYNC-PLAN §4.18: the local set equals the cloud, so every device would write the same copy); nothing was typed for 30 s and the save queue is empty; the tab is not view-only; `now ≥ backup:last.retryAt`; and either a retry is pending or `max(backup:last.at, backup:last.checked)` is older than the interval: **1 hour**, or **3 hours** on a phone (the app's `TOUCH && isMobile()`: no hover and a viewport under 900 px). A tap on «Зробити копію на Диск» or «Зробити копію зараз» ignores the interval and `retryAt` (the hourly cap is for automatic copies) and still skips equal content; the token itself is step 3 of §6.5, so a missing one shows «Копія чекає на дотик» after the hash, not before. The interval is wall-clock time, but the timer runs only in the foreground: hidden tabs are throttled and a backgrounded phone is frozen (§3).

**Retries.** A failed attempt (a network error, a 5xx, a 429, a failed guard update, a full Drive) leaves `checked` as it was and sets `retryAt = now + backoff`, 1 minute doubling to 60 (a full Drive: a day), so the backoff of §6.9 decides the next try and the interval does not swallow it; a successful check clears `retryAt`. **Clocks.** `at`, `checked` and `retryAt` are device time. When `max(at, checked)` is ahead of `now` by more than five minutes, or `retryAt` is further ahead than the longest backoff allows, the clock was set back: `at` and `checked` are clamped to `now`, `retryAt` is cleared, the run's skew is dropped (it was measured against the old clock), and the check is due at once, so a correction of the clock never delays copies by the size of the correction, also after a restart. Within a session the interval is also measured with `performance.now()`, which a clock change does not move; a difference of over five minutes between the two clocks' progress since the last check means a jump in either direction, forward included, and drops the run's skew the same way.

«Only when something changed» is decided by content, not by bookkeeping: at every check that is due the payload is built and hashed (reading 5 000 notes and their `keep` versions once an hour is cheap; B1 measures it, expected under 100 ms). Equal content moves only `checked`; `at` moves only when a copy is confirmed on Drive. The local hash alone may end a check only while `at` is younger than 24 hours; after that the listing of §6.5 decides, so a folder or files the owner deleted by hand are replaced the next day even in a tab that stays open with nothing changed.

### 6.4 What a copy holds: format, names, folder

- The file is the export format of today, gzip-compressed (`application/gzip`), so the current import reads it after decompression and older exports keep importing:

```
{ app: "baobook", format: 1, kind: "backup", site: "app" | "dev", exported: ISO time in UTC,
  device: {id, name},                                   // the writing device, as sync's dev/dn
  settings: {theme, grid, spell},                       // as the export; the import applies them only into an empty app
  notes: [every record of `notes`: trash and archive included, with deleted/archived/revived/pinned/color],
  history: {versions: [records of `versions` with keep: 1, bodies inline], blobs: []} }
```

- The file holds every record of `notes` (§2); the hash and the counts below take only the **synced set**: every note except an empty one that has no cloud base, since the core never queues such a note (`ensureJob`) and it lives on one device only, so two devices in «Синхронізовано» may hold different ones (2026-10-24). Only `keep` records go in: named versions and conflict copies, whose bodies are inline (SYNC-PLAN §4.3), so `blobs` is empty and the automatic history stays per device. The importer ignores `kind`, `site` and `device`. Named versions are the same on every synced device since sync PR 4a-1 sends them (2026-10-24), except the local-only ones («лише тут»: `partial`, `big`, `shape`, SYNC-PLAN §4.9), which stay where they were made; conflict copies (`reason: 'copy'`, never named) are not sent either (SYNC-PLAN §2). So a file's local-only versions and conflict copies are the writing device's, and a device that always finds a fresh copy of another device first never puts its own into any file (§9).
- **The hash** is SHA-256 (`crypto.subtle.digest`) over what sync makes equal on every device: the synced set of notes in canonical form without `updated` (SYNC-PLAN §4.15, §4.4 step 4), each keyed by its id and sorted by id, then the named versions that are not local-only (`meta:v:{id}.local` unset), each projected with a core helper `versionWire(record, base)` that B1 exports next to the internal `vwire` (`note, name, title, blocks` in canonical form, SYNC-PLAN §4.9): the confirmed base `meta:v:{id}.base` when there is one, because a normalized local record may differ from what the cloud holds (a block whose parent area was missing moved to the sheet), else the record; `t` is left out, since a receiving device clamps it to its own clock; keyed by its id and sorted by id (2026-10-24). Two devices in «Синхронізовано» therefore produce one hash whatever the key order of their records and whatever empty new notes, conflict copies and local-only versions each holds, and a change of `updated` alone makes no copy.
- **Names:** `baobook-2026-10-19T14-07.json.gz` on .app and `baobook-dev-2026-10-19T14-07.json.gz` on .dev (a guard copy keeps the original's name with `-guard` before the extension): the writing device's local time (for reading by eye; the ordering uses Drive's `createdTime`), sortable, no colons. Both sites use one OAuth client and see each other's files, so the folders keep them apart: **«Baobook»** for .app, **«Baobook DEV»** for .dev, created in the root of My Drive, found again by `appProperties` (`app = baobook`, `kind = folder`, `site`), the id cached in `backup:folder`. The owner may move or rename the folder; a deleted folder is created again.
- **`appProperties` of a copy:** `app = baobook`, `kind = backup`, `site`, `fmt = 1`, `t` (the writer's clock, ms, informational), `hash` (64 hex), `bytes` (the compressed size), `notes` (the synced set), `blocks` (of those notes), `versions` (named versions that are not local-only), `copies` (conflict copies and local-only versions) as counts, `dev` (device id); optional `guard = 1` (a guard copy, §6.2), `keepUntil` (ms, Drive's time), `keepPending` (a guard waiting for a trusted time) and `reason`. Each under 124 bytes, fewer than 30. A listed file whose `size` differs from its `bytes` is not a copy: it is skipped and deleted.
- **Size.** Notes have no images (SYNC-PLAN §1). 5 000 notes of 1–3 KB are 5–15 MB of JSON and 1–4 MB gzipped; named versions and conflict copies add their bodies. Most copies fit one multipart request (≤ 5 MB); bigger ones take the resumable path.

### 6.5 One copy, step by step

A run captures (epoch, env, uid) and the leadership generation (SYNC-PLAN §4.10, PR 2) at step 1 and checks them again before every Drive write (an upload, an update, a deletion) and inside every IndexedDB transaction that writes a `backup:*` key: the early exits of steps 2 and 5, `retryAt`, the folder cache, the result of a broker probe and the record of step 11 alike. A mismatch (the lock lost on `pagehide` or `freeze`, a change of owner, «Прибрати нотатки з пристрою») ends the run and writes nothing more, as the engine's own epoch checks do (SYNC-PLAN §4.11); a file already uploaded is adopted or pruned by the next run of whichever tab leads.

1. **Read** in one IndexedDB transaction over `notes`, `versions` and `sync`: every note with its `meta:n:` (the synced set of §6.4 needs `base`), the `keep` versions with their `meta:v:` (`local` and `base`), `device`, `owner`, all in that one transaction, so the snapshot is consistent. Build the payload, compute the hash and the counts; `checked = now` by the device clock.
2. **Compare** with `backup:last.hash` while `backup:last.at` is younger than 24 hours: equal → status «Без змін · копія {when}», stop.
3. **Token:** B1 the token of this session, B2 `POST /api/drive/token` (§6.6); none → the waiting status, stop.
4. **Folder:** `files.get` of the cached id (5 units); missing or trashed → `files.list` by `appProperties` (100) → none → create (`files.create`, `mimeType = application/vnd.google-apps.folder`, `appProperties.tz` = this device's zone) and list again: two devices making their first copy at once may both create one, and every device adopts the folder that is first by (`createdTime`, `id`); when more than one folder of the site is found, the children of each other folder are listed once (the `q` of step 5 with that folder's id) and moved into the adopted one with `files.update` (`addParents`/`removeParents`, 50 units each); the other folder is never deleted, since the app cannot see what else the owner put there. The adopted folder's `tz` becomes `policy.tz` (§6.2).
5. **List** the folder (100 a page): `q = '{folder}' in parents and trashed = false and appProperties has {key='app' and value='baobook'} and appProperties has {key='site' and value='{site}'} and appProperties has {key='kind' and value='backup'}`, `fields = nextPageToken, files(id, name, size, createdTime, appProperties)`, `orderBy = createdTime desc`, `pageSize = 100`, every page followed. Drive's fields are compared with Drive's clock: `nowDrive = now + skew`, where `skew` is measured from the `Date` header of the answers **this run** received (the broker's token answer at step 3 in B2, where Cloudflare always sends one; the folder and the listing at steps 4–5) and lives in the run's memory only: it is never stored, so no restart and no clock jump can carry a stale offset into a later run, and a jump within the session drops it (§6.3). With a measured skew a device clock hours off neither stalls nor doubles copies, while `at`, `checked` and `retryAt` stay in the device's time. Without any measurement in this run (B1 on a Drive that sent no `Date`) the run is in **uncertain time**: the device clock stands in, the interval and the 24-hour rule may then be off by the clock's error (a slow clock delays a copy, a fast one allows an early twin that pruning removes), and `prune()` runs with `uncertain`, so no guarded file is released by a clock nobody vouched for, and a guard set now is written as `keepPending` (§6.2). A missing header never makes a copy look fresh. After all pages are loaded the files are ordered by (`createdTime`, `id`), as in §6.2. A file whose `bytes` property is present and differs from its `size` is deleted and ignored. With a measured skew, every `keepPending` file in the listing now gets its `keepUntil = nowDrive + 30 days` (§6.2; a failed update is logged and tried again next run), before either exit below, so the promised conversion does not wait for a change in the notes. **The newest regular copy** (`guard` files aside, §6.2) is the reference for everything that follows: its `hash` equals ours → `at = now`, `backup:last` takes it, stop; its `createdTime` is within the interval by `nowDrive` (automatic runs only; a tap goes on) → another device wrote, `backup:last` takes it, stop. A guard copy is never the reference: one made a minute ago by a run whose upload then failed must not pass for a fresh backup, or the real upload would wait an hour.
6. **Guard:** the shrink check against the newest regular copy (§6.2). When it fires, `files.copy` makes the guard copy of that previous copy now, with its properties set in the same request (`keepUntil` with a measured skew, `keepPending` without), before anything is uploaded; a failure ends the run with `retryAt` (§6.3).
7. **Compress:** `new Blob([json]).stream().pipeThrough(new CompressionStream('gzip'))` → bytes.
8. **Upload:** ≤ 5 MB → one `multipart` request (metadata part with `name`, `parents`, `appProperties`; media part `application/gzip`; `fields = id, name, size, createdTime`). Over 5 MB → resumable: `POST` with the metadata and `X-Upload-Content-Length`, the session URI from `Location` or rebuilt from `X-GUploader-UploadID` (§4), then `PUT` chunks of 8 MiB (a multiple of 256 KiB); on a cut, `PUT` with `Content-Range: bytes */total` asks what arrived and continues; the session is good for a week but the copy is simply redone on the next due check if the tab is gone. **Fallback,** only if B1 proves the browser cannot finish a resumable upload: `PUT /api/drive/upload` streams the bytes through the Worker to Drive with the same metadata; the Worker stores nothing (body limit 100 MB).
9. **Verify** the answer: `id`, `name`, and `size` equal to the bytes sent; otherwise the copy does not count, the file is deleted (and, should that fail, the `bytes` check of step 5 removes it next time) and the row says «Копію не перевірено».
10. **Prune:** `prune(listing + the new file, nowDrive, policy, uncertain)`; `files.delete` each removed file, one at a time, errors logged and retried with the next copy.
11. **Record** `backup:last` {at = checked = now, retryAt cleared, hash, the counts, fileId, name} in a transaction with the context check; status «Копія на Диску · {when}»; the other tabs get `backup {at, state}` over the BroadcastChannel.

### 6.6 Tokens

**B1, one tap.** The row «Резервні копії» offers «Зробити копію на Диск». The tap runs `reauthenticateWithPopup` or `reauthenticateWithRedirect` the way 3b signs in (2026-10-24) (SYNC-PLAN §4.12: `TOUCH || standalone()` picks the redirect, `saveNow()` and `signin: 'pending'` go before it, the SDK is loaded on demand by the same press, a blocked popup says «Продовжити з Google» for the next press) with a `GoogleAuthProvider` carrying `addScope('https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file')` and `login_hint` = the account's email; Google asks for the Drive consent the first time (incremental authorization), and the result's `credential.accessToken` lives about an hour in the leader tab's memory. The copy runs at once, whatever the interval, and the automatic rule may use the same token for another copy within the hour; a new leader (SYNC-PLAN §4.10) asks the other tabs for the token over the BroadcastChannel (same origin) and otherwise waits for a tap; when the token has expired the row says «Копія чекає на дотик» and the next tap repeats this. So B1 is honestly one tap per copy, not one per session: the token lives an hour, and that is what B2 removes.

**B2, the broker in the Worker.** The standard authorization-code flow with a redirect, no Google library:

| Route | Does |
|---|---|
| `POST /api/drive/connect` | `Authorization: Bearer {Firebase ID token}` → verify (§4), uid on the allowlist, the Google `sub` from `firebase.identities["google.com"][0]` and the `email` claim → a self-verifying `state` = base64url of {uid, sub, exp: now + 10 min, nonce} + HMAC-SHA-256 under a key derived from `DRIVE_KEK` → `{url}` of `https://accounts.google.com/o/oauth2/v2/auth` with `client_id`, `redirect_uri = https://{host}/api/drive/callback`, `response_type = code`, `scope = openid email drive.file`, `access_type = offline`, `prompt = consent`, `include_granted_scopes = true`, `login_hint`, `state`. The answer also carries the attempt's `nonce`, which the app stores in `backup:state` before it saves everything and navigates there. No KV is touched. |
| `GET /api/drive/callback` | a `state` whose HMAC or `exp` fails → 400, no network, no KV (a forged flood costs Worker requests only); otherwise `code` exchanged at `https://oauth2.googleapis.com/token` with the client secret. First the account: the `id_token` of the answer (trusted as it came from Google's token endpoint over TLS) must carry the `sub` of the `state`; another account → nothing stored, that grant revoked (the only grants that account can hold to the project are this one and a Firebase sign-in of its own, whose loss costs it nothing), result `wrong-account`. Then the scopes: the answer's `scope` must include `drive.file` (a user may untick it on the consent screen, §4); if not, nothing is stored and **nothing is revoked**, since a revocation would hit every grant of the owner's account to the project (§4), a working broker token included; the previous connection stays as it was and the result is `no-scope` (the unused partial grant retires by the per-client limit or by six months of disuse). No refresh token in the answer → result `retry`; a match → the refresh token encrypted with AES-GCM under `DRIVE_KEK` (a random 256-bit secret; the nonce stored with the ciphertext) → KV `rt:{uid}` = {ct, iv, at}, **overwriting** a previous entry without revoking anything (§4: a revocation would kill the new token and every other grant of the account to the project; the stale token retires by the per-client limit or by six months of disuse), result `ok`. Every outcome is also written to KV `conn:{uid}` = {result, nonce, at} with a 15-minute TTL (the latest attempt wins; the nonce says which attempt it was), and the app learns the outcome from it (below). The page says the outcome in plain words («Диск підключено. Поверніться в Baobook», «Це інший акаунт Google», «Доступ до Диска не надано», «Спробуйте ще раз») and echoes nothing from the query. A replayed callback fails at Google: a code is single-use. |
| `POST /api/drive/token` | ID token verified, uid allowed → KV `rt:{uid}` → `h` = SHA-256 of its ciphertext → KV `dead:{h}` present → 410 without a call to Google; otherwise decrypt → `grant_type = refresh_token` at Google → `{access_token, expires_in}` to the tab, which caches it until a minute before expiry. No entry → 404. Google's `invalid_grant` (revoked, six months unused) → KV `dead:{h}` = {at} with a 30-day TTL → 410. **The token route never deletes `rt:{uid}`:** KV has no compare-and-swap (§4), and a request that read the old token while the callback stored a new one would otherwise delete the new one (the owner reconnects after a revocation, another device's probe fails on the old token a moment later); a dead mark names the ciphertext that failed, so it can never hit a token it did not try. The 410 is answered from Google's `invalid_grant` whether or not the dead mark could be written. Every answer, a 200 included, carries the `conn:{uid}` result when there is one, so a failed reconnect is reported even while an older token still works. |
| `POST /api/drive/disconnect` | ID token verified → KV `dead:{h}` for the current entry, then delete `rt:{uid}` (the owner's own action; a connect racing it is simply redone); with `revoke: true` also revoke at `https://oauth2.googleapis.com/revoke`. |

- **The connection is account state, learned from the broker, not from the page the callback lands on:** the callback may end in another tab, on another device, or inside the in-app browser of the installed iPhone app, which the app never sees. So every leader tab with `mode ≠ broker` asks `/api/drive/token` before the gate of §6.3, at most once in 10 minutes, and once a minute for two minutes after a connect attempt (recorded in `backup:state.since`, since the tab that started it navigated away) (KV may take a minute to show the new entry, and a missing key is cached too, §4); a 200 sets `mode = broker` on that device; a 404 (no entry: disconnected from another device) or a 410 (a dead token: revoked or expired) leaves the mode as it was, except that in `broker` mode it turns to `tap` with the row of that code in §6.9; a `conn` result in any answer whose `nonce` is the one this device stored in `backup:state` shows its own row for 15 minutes («Це інший акаунт Google», «Доступ до Диска не надано», «Підключення не вдалося»), which is how the installed iPhone app, whose callback ended in the in-app browser, learns what happened; other devices ignore results of attempts they did not start. An unfinished attempt (a `nonce` in `backup:state` with no result seen yet) makes the probe run at every check for 15 minutes, whatever the mode and whether or not a copy is due, since a device in `broker` mode with a working old token and unchanged notes would otherwise never ask; after 15 minutes the nonce is dropped. A second device of the account therefore turns automatic on its own, with no second consent.
- **«Відключити Диск»** by default only forgets the token in the Worker (the account's grant stays, and myaccount.google.com can remove it at any time). The dialog offers «також відкликати доступ Baobook у Google» and says what it does: Google revokes per project, so the broker token of the other site and every B1 token on every device die with it, and each site asks to connect again. The Firebase sign-in survives: it has a refresh token of its own.
- The browser receives exactly what Google would give it directly, a one-hour access token; the refresh token and the client secret never leave the Worker; the ciphertext (KV) and the key (a secret) live in different systems, so KV alone yields nothing and the browser reaches the broker only through the Firebase session: its persisted refresh token mints ID tokens, and with them Drive tokens, until «Вийти» (SYNC-PLAN §4.11) or «Відключити Диск» cuts it. The Firebase session is thus the key to the broker, as it already is to the notes in the cloud. Secrets: `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET`, `DRIVE_KEK` via `wrangler secret put`, per Worker (`baobook`, `baobook-prod`). Variables: `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`, `BACKUP_UIDS` (the owner's uid; the same list as the rules). KV namespace `baobook-tokens`, one per Worker. A Cloudflare rate-limiting rule on `/api/*` is set in the dashboard (§8) since the Worker also serves sign-in.
- **Concurrency in KV.** Handlers run in parallel, so no key has a single writer; the keys are laid out so that races are harmless: `rt:{uid}` is written by callbacks only, and of two the later wins while the loser's token retires unused; `dead:{h}` carries the same value from any writer; `conn:{uid}` keeps the latest attempt, named by its nonce; disconnect writes a dead mark and deletes `rt`, and a connect racing it is redone by hand. A KV write may fail or hit the limit of one write a second to one key (§4): a failed dead mark leaves the next request to try Google again and mark again, a failed `conn` leaves the page's own text as the only report, and a failed `rt` write answers the callback with `retry`. The fake KV of §7 models the delay, the limit and the failures.
- Load: one token request a copy, and a probe every 10 minutes while a device is not connected, up to three KV reads a request (`rt`, then `dead` when an entry exists, and `conn` on every answer): under 200 requests and 600 KV reads a day per device; KV writes on connect, on a dead mark and on disconnect only: nothing near the free limits. CPU: RS256 verification, HMAC and AES-GCM through WebCrypto, well under 10 ms.
- The tab prefers the broker when `backup:state.mode = broker`; on a 410 or a failed broker it falls back to the B1 tap. Both sites register their own callback URI on the one OAuth client; the owner's Google account then lists one «Baobook» with Drive access.

### 6.7 With sync and the leader tab

- Only the leader tab backs up: it already runs the engine under the Web Lock and knows the status. Other tabs show the state from the BroadcastChannel and from `backup:last`.
- The gate «Синхронізовано» makes the copy a copy of the account, not of a device: every device in that state holds the same synced set of notes and named versions (§6.4), so their copies hash equal (the hash leaves out the device-local conflict copies, §6.4) and the second is skipped at step 5. A device that is offline or has pending changes makes no copy, and the row says «Копія після синхронізації».
- **Two devices at once.** Drive has no lock and SYNC-PLAN §4.2 allows no other Firestore collections, so there is no claim document; coordination is the listing at step 5 (another copy within the interval → skip) and, when both pass it within seconds, two files a few seconds apart with one hash. The next pruning keeps the newest of the day; the 3-newest rule may keep the twin for a while. Two devices pruning at once compute their sets over different listings: a file one already removed answers 404 to the other (counted as done), and two shrink guards may make two guard copies, which is extra protection, not a loss. A guard is a new file, never an update of one another device may be deleting from an older listing (§6.2). Two folders made at once are resolved at step 4. Every device prunes with the folder's `tz`, so devices in different zones agree on the days, weeks and months (§6.2). Nothing is written twice over the same file, since every copy is a new file.
- A change that lands between the read of step 1 and the upload is in the next copy; a copy is never a merge. A run whose tab loses the lock or its owner stops before its next write (§6.5).
- The import that restores a copy is a local write through the core (SYNC-PLAN §4.14, the import contract), so sync carries the restored notes to the cloud and the other devices as usual. Restored named versions go the same way through `SYNC.importHistory` (sync PR 4a-1; §6.8).

### 6.8 Restore

- The panel «Резервні копії» lists the copies of this site from Drive, newest first: date and time (from `createdTime`, local), size, «{n} нотаток», «захищена до {date}» for a guard copy with a term, «захищена · очікує підтвердження часу» for one still pending (§6.2), and «Зробити копію зараз».
- A chosen copy is downloaded (`files.get?alt=media`, 200 units), decompressed with `DecompressionStream`, parsed and compared with the current notes by id and `updated` and with the `purged:` registry (SYNC-PLAN §4.3): «У копії {n} нотаток: {a} немає на цьому пристрої, {b} новіших у копії, {c} новіших тут, {d} без змін; {e} видалених назавжди повернуться як відновлені; {f} видалених іменованих версій не повернуться», with the titles of the first twenty differing notes. The fifth count matters: by the import contract a note deleted forever since the copy comes back as a revived note on every device (SYNC-PLAN §4.7, §4.14), and the owner should see that before pressing «Імпортувати».
- «Імпортувати» runs the import of today (`importNotes` + `importHistory`): missing notes are added, a note is replaced only by a newer copy and its state before that goes into the history as an `import` version, nothing is deleted. Since sync PR 4a-1 the history import goes through the core (2026-10-24) (`SYNC.importHistory(h, ids)`): a named version is written and queued for the cloud like a note, a conflict copy stays local, and a named version this account deleted (`meta:v:{id}.gone` or a cloud tombstone, SYNC-PLAN §4.9) is skipped, so a deletion made on purpose is not undone by an old copy; the sixth count of the comparison names them. B3 adds what a backup needs on top. Today `SYNC.importHistory` trusts the list of note ids the page passes, and that list comes from memory, where a note whose write failed still sits (the import keeps it as unsaved), so a version could be written for a note that is not in the store; its job would then wait forever for a note that never reaches the cloud, holding «Синхронізовано» and with it every backup. B3 makes `SYNC.importHistory` check the parent note in the `notes` store inside its own transaction and count the versions it skipped for the report (2026-10-24). `importNotes` collects the pairs original id → revived id (`SYNC.importNote` answers `{kind: 'revived', id}`) and re-points the `note` of their versions: a version that was in the cloud went with the purged note (its tombstone job, SYNC-PLAN §4.9) and is skipped as deleted (the sixth count), so only a version that never reached the cloud (local-only, or made while signed out) moves under the revived note, with its local-only limits kept. «Завантажити файл» saves the `.json.gz` on the device.
- The same file imports anywhere: «Імпорт» takes a gzip file (recognised by its first bytes and decompressed in the browser) and plain JSON, so a copy downloaded from Drive by hand restores a fresh Baobook on any device, signed in or not.
- **Without the app:** a copy is gzip around plain JSON. `gunzip`, 7-Zip or the Archive Utility opens it; `notes[].title` and `blocks[].text` hold the text of every block (`html` only carries formatting), so any editor or `jq -r '.notes[] | .title, (.blocks[].text)'` reads the notes. B3 writes a short `README.txt` into the folder that says this (the owner's decision, §2): «Це резервні копії нотаток Baobook. Кожен файл .json.gz — архів gzip із JSON усередині: його відкриває 7-Zip, gunzip чи архіватор системи. Щоб повернути нотатки, виберіть файл у Baobook через „Імпорт“, розпаковувати його не треба. Файли тут створює й видаляє застосунок: він зберігає 7 денних, 4 тижневих і 12 місячних копій.» The text lives in the language files like any UI string, in the language of the device that writes it. The file carries `appProperties` `app = baobook`, `kind = readme`, `site`, so the listing of §6.5 never sees it as a copy, and pruning and the move of strays never touch it. It is written once per folder: while `backup:folder.readme` is not set, a due copy lists the folder for `kind = readme` (100 units) and uploads the file when there is none, then sets the flag; a folder found or created again starts without the flag, so a folder made before B3 gets its README too. A README the owner deleted by hand stays deleted until the folder changes.

### 6.11 Privacy and the consent text

- The copies are unencrypted JSON in the account's own Drive, readable by its owner and by Google, as the notes in Firestore already are. The app asks for `drive.file`, `openid` and `email` only: it sees nothing else in the Drive. Google's consent screen names the Drive permission; in the app, before the first connection: «Baobook створить у вашому Google Диску папку «Baobook» і зберігатиме в ній копії нотаток: щогодини, поки застосунок відкритий, і лише коли щось змінилося. Застосунок бачить лише файли, які створив сам. Відключити можна будь-коли; копії лишаються у вашому Диску, доки ви їх не видалите.»
- B2's Worker keeps an encrypted refresh token and sees access tokens; it never sees a note, unless the upload fallback of §6.5 step 8 is needed, which streams a copy through it without storing it. The copies go to the Drive of the same Google account that signed in to Baobook, never to another one (§6.6). Whoever holds the device's Firebase session can reach the broker (§6.6); signing out ends that.
- «Відключити Диск» forgets the token; revoking the grant at Google is a separate choice, in the dialog or at myaccount.google.com, and acts on the whole project (§6.6). The folder stays until the owner deletes it in Drive. The copies of .dev sit in «Baobook DEV» and may be deleted at any time.

## 9. Risks, open questions and manual checks

- **B1 means one tap per copy,** not one per session: a one-hour token cannot cover the hourly cadence. B2 is the automatic variant; B1 alone is a safe first step and the manual «copy now» later.
- **The CORS of the resumable upload** is the first task of B1 (§4, §7); the fallback through the Worker exists on paper only until it is needed.
- **Google revokes per project** (§4): «Відключити Диск» therefore forgets the token by default and revokes only on request, with a warning that both sites and every B1 token are affected; a reconnect never revokes.
- **The 04:00 rule** is implemented as a backup day boundary, not as a rename at 04:00: nothing runs at 04:00, and the day's last copy stays because the next copy belongs to the next day. **«Local time» is the zone of the account's set of copies,** written into the folder when it is created (§6.2): a device in another zone sees the boundary at another wall-clock hour, and that is the price of every device keeping the same generations; the zone changes only with a new folder.
- **KV has no atomic writes** (§4): the broker's keys are laid out so that every race is harmless (§6.6), the token route never deletes `rt`, and a partial consent revokes nothing; if more shared state ever appears there, a Durable Object is the next step, not more KV keys.
- **Uncertain time** (§6.5): without a `Date` header in this run the device clock decides, which may delay a copy or allow an early twin; no guarded copy is released, and a guard set then has no term until a run with a trusted time gives it one (§6.2); the skew is never stored, so a stale one cannot outlive a clock jump or a restart. B2 ends uncertain time, since the broker's answers carry a `Date`.
- **The shrink guard watches three counts** (notes, blocks, named versions) with the thresholds of `SHRINK`, agreed by the owner on 2026-10-19 (§2), and keeps the previous copy as a guard copy of it (the same bytes under a new file, §6.2), since protecting the file in place races with other devices' deletions; the **30 s quiet period**, the **24-hour limit** of the local-hash skip, the **two-minute** broker retry, the **10-minute** probe and **`maxRemove = 3`** remain the plan's proposals, constants with tests.
- **Answered on 2026-10-19:** a `README.txt` in the folder (§6.8), and relative time in the status row, with dates and times in the list of copies (§6.9).
- **Google storage full** stops copies, not the app; the row says so and retries daily.
- **Revocation and the six-month rule** end the broker's token: the row asks to connect again; nothing is lost.
- **The hash and the counts take the synced set** (§6.4): an empty new note without a cloud base, a local-only named version and a conflict copy live on one device only, so they are in the file but not in the hash or the shrink counts; otherwise two devices in «Синхронізовано» would alternate copies of equal content (2026-10-24).
- **Conflict copies travel only with the device that writes a file:** they are not synced, the hash leaves them out, and a device that always finds another device's fresh copy first never puts its own into any file. The decision to include them holds for the writing device; `copies` in the properties shows how many a file holds.
- **A restore of an old copy revives notes deleted forever since,** everywhere, by the import contract; the comparison names how many before the import (§6.8).
- **Only the owner's uid** may use the broker, and only an account the rules accept ever reaches the gate of B1; every other account gets the manual export as today.
- **Manual checks** (written as items of a `docs/BACKUP-CHECKLIST.md`, like `SYNC-CHECKLIST.md` (2026-10-24)): the consent screens on both hosts (the app name and the Drive line; no «unverified» warning for non-sensitive scopes), the redirect flows on an iPhone in Safari and in the Home Screen app (the owner uses both, §2), a phone that stays open in front for three hours, a copy over 5 MB, Drive's folder renamed and moved by hand (the copies still land in it), the folder deleted by hand (recreated), the Google account page showing «Baobook» with Drive access and revoking it, a wrong account in the consent screen, a consent screen with the Drive permission unticked.
