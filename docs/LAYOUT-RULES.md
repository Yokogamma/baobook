The review log has the details. It gathers every decision the owner has made on placing and moving blocks up to this date, from §2 of [`LAYOUT-MODEL.md`](LAYOUT-MODEL.md) and from the trials in the mockup, and cuts them into modules with explicit boundaries, so that the new engine can be written and tested rule by rule. ## 0. How to read

- Every rule has an id, `MODULE.n`, and says what must hold, not how a function does it. After it come the decisions it rests on, in square brackets, and, where useful, the mockup function, in round brackets.
- A module owns its rules. A rule never reads or writes the state of another module except through the inputs and outputs in the module's header.
- **One solver.** Every change of the layout, whatever the user did, goes through one function, SETTLE (§5): a gesture or an edit only describes its change (§5.2), and SETTLE runs its phases in their fixed order. No gesture carries its own pushing code. Rows follow sizes (FOLLOW) and gaps are found again (RELINK) at named points of SETTLE, never in between.
- Numbers the owner set are settings or constants with a name (§1.5).
- **Switchable rules** [R1]. Every rule that is not core can be turned off without breaking the others; §1.6 lists them, with what replaces each one when it is off and the rules it needs. The engine takes the set of rules that are on as an input; a rule that is off is skipped or replaced by its fallback, and the rules that need it go off with it. Each rule's tests run with it on and off.

## 1. Vocabulary

### 1.1 Space

- **Cell.** The grid step `G`, 24 px at 16 px text. Dots of the grid sit on cell corners. Every size and threshold is counted in cells; a larger text size scales the sheet, and where the blocks' insets, fixed in pixels, make a text wrap otherwise, that is a re-measure (LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN §4.4).
- **Container.** The sheet, or an area. Each has its own columns `0 … cols−1` and rows `0, 1, …`. An area's `cols` is its width; the sheet's is `SHEETW` (§1.5), stored with the note, the same on every device, never the window's width [W7].
- **Block.** A rectangle of cells in one container: column `col`, row `y`, width `w` in cells, height `h` in rows. Every block's card is at least one cell wide: an empty text's natural width is one cell, and a placeholder is drawn but never measured (PR-8).
- **Handle cell.** The first cell of a block, column `col`, holds the grip ⋮⋮. It is not part of the block for collisions [M4].
- **Card.** The rest of the block: columns `col+1 … col+w−1`, rows `y … y+h−1`. The card holds the content.
- **Shares a column.** Two blocks share a column when their cards have a column in common: `a.col+1 < b.col+b.w` and `b.col+1 < a.col+a.w`; for an area, its extent stands for its card.
- **Shares a row.** Their row ranges intersect: `a.y < b.y+b.h` and `b.y < a.y+a.h`.
- **Extent.** What a block takes in its container for every rule that compares it with another block there: a block's card; an area's frame with one free cell on every side for its handles (AREA.7a), columns `col … col+w` and rows `y−1 … y+h`. Sharing a column or a row, hitting, reaching, `sink`, P-SEP, the stacks and FOLLOW (an area stands in its chains by its extent) and `open()`'s separation all compare extents, so nothing comes into an area's free cell; two areas keep two free cells between them, one for each one's handles (proposed). An area's stored and shown place is its frame; its frame stands at row 1 or lower, and its right free column at its container's last column at most, so that its free row above and its free column on the right lie in its container (proposed; `open()` moves an area at row 0 down one row, LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN §3.5). In GAP an area is its extent: its top one row above its frame and its canonical height its frame's plus two, so its lend (G7 caps it at the extent's height), its room, its slack, a fresh stack's rows and what its body leaves when it narrows off a column are all counted in rows of its extent. One set of geometry functions computes all of these (LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN §2).
- **Hit.** Their extents share a column and a row. Two blocks of one container never hit (INV-1).
- **Reading order.** By row, then by column, then by id. Every rule that visits several blocks visits them in reading order of the layout its phase starts from (INV-5).
- **Neighbour order.** Every upper neighbour before its lower neighbours; ties by reading order. The rules that visit blocks under one another use it; FOLLOW walks the column chains (§1.3).

### 1.2 Sizes

- **Elastic block.** A text or a code block whose width was not set by hand. Its width follows its content (SIZE.1); the two kinds follow the same rules [W14].
- **Set width** `cw`. A width set with the width handle. Kept as is (SIZE.3).
- **Set height** `ch` and **fold**. One handle at the bottom edge writes one of them (RESIZE.3): released above the content it writes `ch`, a spare room below the text; released below the content it writes `fold`, the rows shown [H1].
- **Shown height** `h`. The rows the block takes now: its content rows at its width, or `ch` when larger, or `fold` plus one row for «Розгорнути» when the content is longer, minus the empty lines an area hid (SIZE.6). It also changes with **temporary heights**, which are a second measure and never stored (§2.1): «Розгорнути» until the note is closed [H3], the unfolding while the caret is in a folded block [H4], `FOLDPHONE` in the narrow view [H6], the provisional empty line of EDIT.3, the row of the wide field under every block while that setting is on (P18).
- **Natural width.** The width of the longest line of a text, plus the inner padding, in whole cells.
- **Rest width.** The width an elastic block has when nothing carries or presses it (SIZE.1).

### 1.3 Neighbours, gaps and stacks

- **Upper and lower neighbour.** For a block `b`, a block `a` of the same container is an upper neighbour when, in at least one column of `b`'s card, `a` is the nearest block above `b` (`a.y+a.h ≤ b.y`); `b` is then a lower neighbour of `a`. Neighbours are found from the places whenever a rule needs them (P-DOWN, P-UP, phase 11); they are not stored.
- **Column chain.** In one column of a container, the blocks whose cards cover it, top to bottom. Each of them is, in that column, either an **anchor**, which has a stack there, or a **standing block**, which stands in the stack of the first anchor below it. The last block of a chain is always an anchor; the first may stand, when it was put into the free room above the block under it (GAP.5 rule 3).
- **Stack** (GAP). For a block `l` and a column where it is an anchor: the anchor above it in that column, `u`, or the top of the container; the gap's rows from `u`'s bottom (or the top) down, in order, each with its owner or unowned; and, among them, the blocks standing in that gap, in order, each with its **lend**, the rows of the gap its body covers by its canonical height (§2.1). A stack is the whole gap that `l` had under `u` before anything came between them; the blocks that came between stand in it and do not cut it. That is what keeps every gap whole whatever comes in and goes out (G7, S8).
- **Gap.** The rows of a stack: runs from top to bottom, cut by the blocks standing in it into **segs**, a seg being the rows between two neighbours of the chain; `{owner, n}`, `n` rows that belong to block `owner`, or `{nobody, n}`, `n` unowned rows. Every owner covers the column of the rows it owns. Unowned rows are free room: the rows above the first block of a column, under the top; they belong to no block, so taking a block away never takes them (GAP.6) [G1, G3]. A block's rows may lie under it (its gap, the rows of the stack under it that it owns, and the rows G5 and G6 put under it) and above it (its room, below).
- **Lend and room.** A standing block's lend is never more than its height (G7): when it really shrinks below it, the rows it no longer covers leave the gap; a temporary height that ends is not a shrink. A block put lower than the gap reaches (the block under it held lower by another column) owns the rows between the end of the gap's rows and its top, its **room**: they lie above it and go with it.
- **Head block.** A block that is the first of the chain in every column of its card: in each, an anchor whose stack starts at the top with nothing standing in it, or the first block standing in a stack that starts at the top. `isHead(layout, block)` is the one test of it that every rule uses, so that a later kind of block (a pinned block, O-7) can be a head block by it; it needs the layout, since a standing block's stacks are not its own. GAP.8 asks something else, per column: whether a block is the first there (G6). A head block's row is not trusted on its own: it comes from its stacks like any other (INV-2).
- **Can move.** `canMove(block, direction)`: whether a primitive may move a block that way. In the first release every block can move every way within its container; a block that cannot is an obstacle (§5.3).
- **Binding stack.** Of a block's stacks, the one that puts it lowest decides its row (INV-2); the others are kept as they are, with **slack** (the rows by which they do not hold it), and take over when the binding one goes.

### 1.4 The target of a gesture

- **Target.** What a pointer move asks for, after DRAG.A: the column, the row, the fractional row `frow` (the row of the block's top before rounding, equal to the row when the row is held by sticking), the width, and the state: «move», «home» or «outside».

## 2. State and invariants

### 2.1 What is stored and what is derived

**Stored**, for every block: its container, `col`, `y`, its sizes set by hand (`cw`, `ch`, `fold`), the count of empty lines an area hid (SIZE.6); and, for every column of its card where it is an anchor, its stack (§1.3): `u`, the gap's runs, and the blocks standing in it with their lends. Adjacent columns nearly always have equal stacks, so they are kept by stretches of columns (LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN §2.1). Everything about the rows above a block is on that block, the lends of the blocks standing in them included.

**Derived**, never trusted over the stored state: every block's shown width and height (SIZE); every block's row, a head block's too, given by INV-2 and recomputed by FOLLOW, the stored `y` being a cache; the neighbours (§1.3).

So a temporary height (§1.2) moves the blocks under it on the screen and writes nothing: the stacks stay, and when the temporary height ends the blocks are back [H3, H4, H6, G1]. That holds for a temporary height that starts and ends with no move in its reach between them: a move under it is made on the screen, and the slack it takes stays the moved block's own rows, so the blocks stay where they were seen when it ends (GAP.5 rule 1, O-22) [G10]. A window, a scale, the narrow view and the dev look write nothing either (INV-6) [W2, W6, V1]. How the stacks are written into a note and merged by sync is E-1 (§15).

**One layout, two measures.** The *canonical* layout is the stored state with its derived rows, measured with no temporary height. The *displayed* layout is the same layout measured with the temporary heights: the same stacks and widths, its rows given by FOLLOW with that measure; nothing else changes. The user aims at what is displayed, so a gesture, its memory and every SETTLE of a gesture or an edit run on the displayed layout, and the result is shown as it is (INV-8). What is stored is the result's canonical layout: FOLLOW with the canonical measure, its stacks and widths kept. Taking them out moves blocks only up, keeping the order of the blocks in every column, so no two blocks come to hit; putting them back gives the shown result again. The displayed layout is made container by container, the areas' children first: an area's temporary rows and its children's give its shown height, which its container's FOLLOW then uses, as §5.4 hands an area's growth to its container. A temporary height does not start or end while a gesture is held; one that would waits for the gesture's end [H3, H4, P18]. The stacks are the same in both measures, and a temporary height starting or ending changes none: it pushes the blocks under its block on the screen, as a growth would, whatever that block is, and writes nothing. A standing block's lend is measured by its canonical height, so a block put into a gap while it shows taller (a carry of an unfolded block, a drop with P18's row) covers its canonical rows there and pushes the blocks under it by its temporary rows on the screen only; taken away, it gives every row back. What the user aims at is measured on the screen: the target of a carry, from the blocks above it there, and a move, which rule 1 makes on the displayed layout as if the temporary heights were real, so that with them taken out it holds where a gap holds the block, as after a growth that shrinks back (H1). A change of columns is written at the canonical rows, so that no push of a temporary height is written (GAP.5): a block that comes into a column by itself (widened, pressed aside) takes its canonical row there, unless the screen put it under a block of that column (rule 3); rule 2 keeps a narrowed anchor's canonical body and slack. In the views where blocks are placed a temporary height only adds rows: `FOLDPHONE` belongs to the narrow view, which has no stacks (VIEW.2).

### 2.2 Invariants

The engine keeps these after every SETTLE; each module's tests check them.

- **INV-1 No overlap.** Two blocks of one container never hit.
- **INV-2 Rows follow the stacks.** Every block's row is the highest that the stacks of all its columns allow. In each column a cursor walks the chain from the top: it adds each seg of rows, puts each standing block and each anchor at least at the cursor, and goes on from where the block actually stands, plus its height in the measure in use (§2.1). A head block's row comes from its stacks too.
- **INV-3 Stacks follow the columns** [G3]. In every column the chain of anchors and the blocks standing in their stacks are exactly the blocks covering it, in row order; a stack's `u` is the anchor above it in that column, or the top; no lend is longer than its block's canonical height, in every layout a RELINK gives (between two RELINKs only rule 2 reads a lend, GAP.4); every owned row's owner covers that column, so no block that has gone leaves rows behind; no stack puts its block lower than its row. There is no gap under the last block of a column.
- **INV-4 Inside the container.** Every card lies within its container's columns; every block of an area lies within the area's rows (§8).
- **INV-5 Determinism.** The same stored state and the same measured sizes give the same layout on every device, whatever order the blocks are stored in. Every rule that visits several blocks visits them in reading order of the layout its phase starts from; ties go to the upper block, then to the left one.
- **INV-6 Only user actions write.** The stored state changes only by the changes of §5.2 that the user makes: a drop, an arrow, a handle, creating or deleting a block, typing, putting the caret into a block that hides empty lines; and by what SETTLE does in consequence of them. A window, a scale, the narrow view, the looks, the rules list (VIEW.8) and the temporary heights write nothing [W2, W6, V1, R1]. Nor does a block that measures differently with no user changing it (a linked note renamed changes a chip's label; a font loads late): its new size is shown at once, and what it moves is written with the next user action in that container (LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN §3.4). One exception, not a user action: the first time a computer opens a note that has no `SHEETW` yet, it stores its own (VIEW.1c) [W7].
- **INV-7 No trace.** (a) Within one carry, «home» gives back the layout the carry started from [C1]. (b) A block inserted and then taken away leaves its neighbours as they were: two blocks that touched touch again, a gap that was there is there again [C3]; the same for a block that came between two blocks by itself and then went, or narrowed back off their columns (GAP.5 rule 3) [S8], for a block put in while it showed taller and taken away after its temporary height ended (§2.1), and for a text that grew and shrank back, whether the stacks beside it bind their blocks or not [H1]; a standing block that really shrank below the rows it covered gives back only what it still covers (G7). This is for the blocks that were its neighbours when it came, and for the gaps as they were then. A block moved by hand meanwhile changes the gaps on purpose (G5, G6). A block that comes into a column or leaves it while the inserted block is in, where that block, or what it pushed, stands, takes its place there as it finds it (S8, G8) and keeps it when the inserted block goes, which is G2 for it. So a block that came into its gap stands in it (G4), and stands on in the gap of the block under it when it goes, where without the inserted block it would have come in as an anchor: deleting it later gives its lend back instead of closing its gap. A block that came into a column while the inserted block's growth held it, or the blocks of that column, lower keeps that row there. An anchor narrowed off a column keeps as rows only the slack the inserted block's growth left it (G8), so the block under it rises by what the growth had taken when the inserted block goes. (c) Carrying one block back and forth between the same two places, or moving several blocks together in one step and back, puts every block back where it was, and doing it any number of times never makes a gap longer than the first round trip made it; that first one may lengthen a stack that did not hold the block by the slack it used, so where a growth or a temporary height held the block meanwhile, it may stand that much lower once that shrinks back or ends [G4]. A single move up under one does the same to the blocks under the moved one: the slack it used becomes its own rows under it, which hold them that much lower in the stored layout until it moves back (rule 1).
- **INV-8 The preview is the result.** What any gesture shows while it is held (a carry, a width or height handle, an area's handle) is exactly what letting go stores, as it is displayed (§2.1): the rows FOLLOW gives after the last RELINK, pushes from a block that came into a column at its canonical row included (GAP.5 rule 5). Nothing moves after the release.

## 3. Module SIZE: how big a block is

Inputs: a block's content, its set sizes, its container's columns, the blocks on its rows to its right, `ORDER`, `MINC`, `TEXTW`. Outputs, as answers to three questions: the block's rows at a given width; the narrowest it may get (none for a block that never narrows); the empty lines at the start and end of its text that may hide. And the rest width of every elastic block. SIZE owns the hidden-lines count; other modules change it only through a change of §5.2. SIZE knows nothing about gestures. What a block measures depends on its content as it is shown (a chip shows the linked note's current name), its kind's options (a code block's wrap and line numbers, an area's title), the text size, the font and the text placement (P18); a change of any of them changes the block's size (phase 1a), whoever made it, and INV-6 says what is written.

- **SIZE.1 Rest width.** A code block's natural width is its longest line, and it is never narrower than its kind's minimum, which its passport gives (its header's 12 cells, `MINW_CODE`, as today); otherwise it follows this rule as a text does; a line longer than its width wraps or scrolls sideways inside the block by its own «перенос» setting, as today [W14]. An elastic text is as wide as its natural width, at most `TEXTW`, and no wider than the room it has: the room ends at its container's right edge and at the handle cell of the nearest block to its right on the rows it takes at that width. It is never narrower than `min(natural width, MINC)`. When room frees up, it widens again by itself [W3]. (`autoCard`)
- **SIZE.2 Giving way.** Pressed from either side, an elastic block gives way the same way, by `ORDER`: it moves aside as a whole while there is room and narrows where it meets the edge of its container or a block that cannot move; or it never narrows. It narrows on the side it is pressed from, down to `min(natural width, MINC)` [W3, W10]. What a block that can neither move nor narrow does is SETTLE's (P-ASIDE, phase 9). (`elastic.min`, `elastic.first`, `shove`)
- **SIZE.3 Set width.** A block with `cw` keeps it and is only moved, never narrowed [W3, W4]. A double click on the width handle removes `cw` (RESIZE.2).
- **SIZE.4 «Never narrow» at rest.** The rest width is the same under every `ORDER`: with «never narrow» too, a close neighbour on the right caps it (SIZE.1). «Never narrow» only changes SIZE.2, how the block gives way when pressed [W9].
- **SIZE.5 (dropped).** «Narrow first» is no longer an order [W10].
- **SIZE.6 Height.** The shown height as in §1.2; nothing ever scrolls inside a block; a text's lines are one cell tall, so a fold always falls between two lines [H1–H6]. `ch` below the content is ignored: a block whose content outgrows its `ch` grows (RESIZE.3).
- **SIZE.7 Hidden empty lines.** A block whose kind hides lines (its passport says so: in 0.2 an elastic text) with no size set by hand may hide the empty lines at the start and at the end of its text; the lines between paragraphs never hide. Only an area's top or bottom handle hides them (P-SQUEEZE); the count is stored, the text never changes. The end lines hide first. They come back when the caret is put in the block (EDIT.5) or when the area grows again (AREA.5) [W5].
- **SIZE.8 Width and height decide each other.** A text's rest width depends on which neighbours cross its rows, and its rows depend on its width. SIZE settles this in reading order, each block once per pass, in `RESTPASSES` passes, the same on every device (E-2).

## 4. Module GAP: stacks

Inputs: a **transition**, what SETTLE declares it did between two RELINKs, and the rules that are on (§1.6). (Step 1b, proposed: the lab model `tests/lab/gap-model` runs it, `relink(T)`, and `declare.mjs` checks it; the engine follows in step 1b's second part.) GAP reads what happened from the transition and never infers it from the geometry. Three words first: a block **put at a place** comes into a column at a row SETTLE chose for it, on the screen (the carried block, a created one, a block that passes another) or by its canonical row (an undo's carry back); a block that comes in **by itself** (widened, pressed in aside) comes in at its **reference**, the canonical row it would have kept with its columns (GAP.5, R); a neighbour is **pinned where it stopped** when phase 3 holds it at the row it had reached, pushed or not (§5.1b). A transition holds:

- `base`, the layout the SETTLE compares with (§5.1), and `after`, the layout after its phases: both displayed, every block with its columns, its height, the rows of it that are temporary and its canonical row (LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN §2.1). The rows of `after` are targets: where SETTLE put each block on the screen; an undo's carry back also has its canonical target. RELINK measures lends by the canonical height.
- `steps`: the moves the change, the primitives, the pin and the swap made, in order, each a **rigid** step (every block it moves moves by the same rows), **one step per common part**: a block a move pushes moves in the same step as that move, over the rows it moves with it, so that the steps replayed in order never put a block on another nor carry it past one. A carry from row 0 to 7 that pushes the block under it from row 4 to 8 is the carried block alone over the gap, then both: `[b3] +3`, `[b3, b1] +4`; P-UP taking A from row 2 to 0 and B from 6 to 1 is B up 3, then A and B up 2. The blocks that follow a move with their gaps (`sink`'s lower neighbours) are in its step. SETTLE builds the steps from the rows the change and the primitives moved each block, after its phases, cut by common parts: the blocks moved down and those moved up form separate groups, in the order of the phases that moved them, and in a group the move alone comes first over the rows it moves alone, then with each block it pushes. A press aside moves no row and is no step: it is a leave and an entry (`press`, `self`). FOLLOW after a change of height, and the heights themselves, are never steps; a step's canonical displacement can be smaller than its displayed one (a block a temporary height pushes, held canonically by another column), so a step's rows are never added to a canonical row.
- `start`: the rows the first step starts from, FOLLOW's over `base`'s stacks with the heights of `after`, for the blocks a change of height or of temporary rows reaches (the blocks under such a block, in its columns, and under those); every other block starts from its row in `base`. SETTLE gives it for every block such a change reaches, and GAP checks it against its stacks.
- `carried`: the carried block, if any, an undo's carry back marked as such.
- For every block and every column it leaves, **why**: `away` (an undo's carry back, in every column it had; a block taken away entirely is one `after` does not hold, GAP.6, and declares nothing), `narrow` (narrowed off it, or left by the carried block while it keeps other columns, M9) or `press` (pressed off it aside).
- For every block and every column it comes into, **how**: `put`, at its displayed target (the carried block, or a block `base` does not hold); `self`, by itself, at its reference (widened, or pressed in aside); `back`, at its canonical target (an undo's carry back, in every column it has, those it keeps included: it leaves them all as carried away and comes back into each; and a deleted block an undo creates again at its old cell, LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN §6.4, declared as the carried block).
- `swap`: the carried block and its neighbour, when phase 3 swapped them (§5.1b), and `pass`: for a block of the swap and a column it keeps where its order with another block changes, that it passes there. In a column the two share, the carried block passes the neighbour, which counts as a block that stayed and moved; the neighbour may pass another block in a column of its own the carried block does not cover (a neighbour shorter than the carried block). A carry inside its columns changes no order: every neighbour keeps its side and is pushed (DRAG.B2, «Reaches»); only a swap changes an order, or the fallback of a switched-off rule that puts a neighbour on the carried block's other side (`P-UP` off: a block judged above is pushed down under it; `DRAG.B2.keep` off: sides by `frow` and the middle), declared as `fallback`, where the carried block passes that neighbour as in a swap. At a swap the neighbour moves alone: the blocks that followed it when it was pushed stay where the push put them, and it lands where the swap put it, temporary heights or not (O-21) [G9]. The pin and the swap are two things: the pin is a move, by which the carried block uses its gap up as any block moved down does (G6); the swap then exchanges the two blocks' places, each keeping its own rows (G4). An undo's carry back keeps no place in any chain (it leaves every column it had and comes back into every column it has), so it passes nothing.

GAP checks a transition against its geometry before it runs it, and a transition that fails the check is an internal error of SETTLE: RELINK refuses it, writes nothing, and SETTLE ends with `{failed}` (§5.5). (In the lab model the check is `declare.mjs`, run by its callers; `relink(T)` itself refuses only a change of order no pass explains, and a result that breaks an invariant.) The check: every column a block leaves or comes into is declared, and nothing else; every change of order of two blocks in a column both keep is a declared pass, and every declared pass changes an order there; a swap is the carried block and the next block over or under it in a column they share, their order exchanged, at the rows §5.1b gives (the neighbour pinned where it stopped, after a push or not, its swap its last step: going down, it moves up by the carried block's displayed height and the carried block stands right under it; going up, the carried block takes its top row and it moves down right under the carried block); no two blocks share a cell in `after` (INV-1); from the rows the first step starts from (`start` for the blocks a change of height reaches, `base` for the others; a transition with no `start` for a block a change of height reaches is refused, since its steps could not be checked), the steps add up to every moved block's row in `after`, replayed in order put no block on another nor carry it past one without a declared pass (the two blocks of a swap, and a block and the one it passes, go through each other; an undo's carry back takes no part), and, with nothing taken away, nothing leaving a column and no undo, a block no step moves keeps its row; a change of height elsewhere never turns this check off; and every reason is one its block may give (only an undo's carry back leaves `away`, the carried block leaving the columns it no longer covers `narrow` (M9); only the carried block comes in `put`, besides a created one; only an undo comes in `back`, a deleted block it makes again included, never `put`; only a block of a swap, or the carried block under a declared fallback, passes). The geometry cannot tell every reason (a block that leaves a column may be narrowed or carried away), so SETTLE's own tests check the reasons it gives (step 2).

Outputs: the stacks and the derived rows; `yc`, with an area's temporary rows, is refreshed by every FOLLOW in SETTLE. GAP is the layout's memory and the only module that writes stacks. Its operations are FOLLOW and RELINK; SETTLE calls them at named points. `base` holds the stacks of `start`, with the places phase 1 gave (§5.1). The executable model `tests/lab/gap-model` implements this section and runs its examples; where the two differ, it is a bug of one of them.

- **GAP.1 Stacks from places.** `open()` builds every stack from the places alone: in every column, top to bottom, every block is an anchor whose stack holds the rows from the block above (or the top) to it, owned by the block above, unowned under the top. Nothing stands in any stack yet. (`open`)
- **GAP.2 Whose rows.** Every row of a gap has an owner or is unowned (§1.3). A gap's rows are, by default, the property of the block above them, and move with that block in its columns; blocks that swap places keep their own rows [G1, G4]: the neighbour keeps its gap, and the carried block, which used its gap up when it was pinned against the neighbour (G6), leaves what it may still have there, in a column where another column held it, as rows of the stack they lie in (rule 2's pass; step 1b, proposed).
- **GAP.3 Only between blocks.** There is no gap under the last block of a column: a stack always ends at its block [G3].
- **GAP.4 FOLLOW.** Every row is recomputed by INV-2, in the measure in use (§2.1): the blocks under a block whose shown height changed follow with their gaps and come back when it shrinks. FOLLOW changes no stack, a temporary height starting or ending included, and the rows it moves are not moves for RELINK [H1, G1]. A block's sizes are set only through `resize`, which sets its height and its temporary rows together from the measure's two answers, in every phase that sets a size (1a, 1c, 4, every pass of SIZE.8 in 9 (a), P-REST in 9 (b), `open()`). G7 is the GAP operation `cap`: a standing block that really shrinks below its lend gives up the rows it no longer covers, from the bottom of its lend. RELINK runs it, and nothing else does: after rule 2, against the canonical heights of `after` (GAP.5). A shrink is real when a RELINK sees it: a block whose canonical height in `after` is below its lend in `base`. FOLLOW counts segs and heights and never reads a lend, and between two RELINKs a lend is read only by rule 2 (GAP.6 in phase 1b takes a block away with the lend `start` gave it); so a height that falls and rises again between two RELINKs (a text widened in a pass of SIZE.8 and narrowed again in phase 9 (b)) leaves no trace, as a growth that shrinks back leaves none (H1), while one that a RELINK's `after` still has lower is cut, whichever phase lowered it [G7]. (`follow`, `resize`, `cap`)
- **GAP.5 RELINK.** `relink(transition, rules)` (step 1b, proposed; until then `relink(base, after, moves, carried, rules)`, its geometry reading who passed whom). A block of `base` that `after` no longer holds is taken away first, by GAP.6, as phase 1b does. Then RELINK runs the transition on three layouts made from `base`:
  - **R, the reference**: the same transition with no change of columns. Every block keeps its columns of `base`, the carried block only those it keeps and an undo's carry back none; a block that passes another leaves, as rule 2's pass, every one of them where its order changes; then the heights of `after`, `cap` and rule 1; then the passers come back there at their displayed targets; then rule 5. A block's canonical row in R is its **reference**: where it comes into a column by itself (rule 3), whatever columns it leaves.
  - **W, the result**: rule 2 for every declared leave and pass, column by column, top to bottom in each chain of `base`, by what `base` gave the block there; then the heights and columns of `after`; `cap` (GAP.4) for every block still standing in a column whose lend is longer than its canonical height in `after`, so that a height a phase lowered after `base` was made counts (phase 9 (a) after phase 8), and a block that leaves some columns gives back there what it had; rule 1 for every step; then the blocks put at a place come in, top to bottom: the carried block and a created one at their displayed targets, an undo's carry back by its canonical row, a block that passes another back into the columns it passed in, at its displayed target (rules 3 and 4).
  - **M, where the blocks that come in by themselves are measured**: W with rule 5 made, nothing of it written, so that a block rule 5 is to lower is measured where it will stand.

  Then every block that comes in by itself comes into W, top to bottom, at its reference, measured against the canonical rows of M; one that came in before it in this RELINK is measured at its own reference (blocks that come in at once are one path, G4). Then rule 5 and FOLLOW give the result. A change of columns is thus measured where the rest of the change puts the blocks, and each block's own row is the one it would have kept with its columns. W holds no column a block has left; R, where every block keeps its columns of `base` and blocks may overlap, serves only each block's own reference; a transition in other columns changes nothing of either. Rule 1's owners (`GAP.5.1`) and rules 2 and 3 can be switched off on their own, and with them `GAP.8` and `GAP.9` (§1.6); rule 1's lengths of gaps and rules 4 and 5 are core.
  1. **adjust** (blocks that stayed and moved). For each step of `moves`, in order, the blocks of the chain that the step moved and that are next to one another in it form **runs**. Inside a run nothing changes: the gaps between its blocks, owners included, and the lends of the blocks standing in it go along, so blocks moved together keep the gaps between them. Only the gap just above each run and the gap just under it change, each once. A gap **holds** the run's top when it has no slack there, slacks measured on the screen at the rows the step starts from (for the first step, the rows FOLLOW gives over `base`'s stacks with the heights of `after`, a height a phase changed being no move, while the gaps are those rule 2 left: a temporary row of a block that left the column no longer above the moved block is slack there, O-22; for every later step likewise: every block at the row it starts from plus the steps that moved it so far, whatever the departures of rule 2 freed, so the result does not depend on how a move is cut into steps): the move is made on the displayed layout as if the temporary heights were real (§2.1), and with them taken out the canonical layout keeps it where its gaps hold the block, as after a growth that shrinks back (H1); the slack a block used moving up under a temporary height stays its own rows under it, so the blocks under it stand that much lower once the temporary height ends. A run moved down by `d`: the gap under it loses `d` rows, first rows of the run's blocks wherever they lie in it, then rows from its top (G6: «moved down, it uses that gap up first»). The gap above it grows by `d` rows owned by its stack's `u` (unowned under the top) where it holds the run's top, and keeps its rows where it does not. When the run stands in that stack and so does the gap under it, the gap above grows instead by as many rows of `u` as the gap under it lost, whatever the slack, so that the stack keeps every row it had, and, where it holds the run's top, by the rest as the run's top's room, its own rows. A run moved up by `k`: in the gap above it the slack goes first, then rows from its bottom. Under the run, at the top of the gap under it, come first the slack used, as rows of the run's top, then the rows it left, with their owners (G5). In a column where the run's top is the first block, the free room it leaves becomes its own rows too (G6, S7); with `GAP.8` off, that free room and the slack it used there stay free room. With nothing under the run in that column, the rows go [G5, G6, S7]. The neighbour's swap step (§5.1b) is structural (O-21): it moves the neighbour by the carried block's displayed height on the screen and writes none of the carried block's temporary rows between the two. Going up, in a column the carried block comes into over it, what the gap above the neighbour gains is less by those rows: where it holds it, the step less them, and where the neighbour stands in one stack with the gap under it, the rows that gap lost, whatever the slack, less them; so the carried block, coming in there with its canonical body, pushes it by them on the screen only. In a column of its own, both gaps round it change by the step less those rows, the carried block's canonical height: the step is as any move there but for the carried block's temporary rows, which are not written under it either, so a block under it there is pushed by them on the screen only while they last (the owner, 2026-11-10, G9). Going down, in a column the carried block comes into under it, the rows that come under the neighbour are that many fewer: the temporary rows the carried block's pass kept at its old place (rule 2) are taken out as it comes back; in a column the carried block does not come into, the neighbour's step is as any move: what it moves up through stays its own rows under it. When the carried block's temporary rows end, the neighbour follows it. A move cut into steps gives what one step gives, owners and lends included: under a run moved up, the slack all its steps of one RELINK used comes first, then the rows they left, in order; over a run moved down in one stack with the gap under it, the rows of `u` first, then its room. So a carry and its way back put every block where it was (INV-7 c), and a text that grows and shrinks beside a stack leaves it as it was, FOLLOW being no move. (`move`)
  2. **leave** (a block left the column: deleted, carried away, narrowed off it, pressed off it sideways, or passed: a block of a swap leaving its place in the chain of a column it keeps, to come back at its new one). A block that stops covering a column while it keeps others was narrowed or pressed off it, the `carried` block too (a carry sideways leaves the columns it no longer covers as a narrowing does, the owner, 2026-11-10, M9); only an undo's carry back leaves its columns as carried away, and a block taken out of all its columns is taken away by GAP.6. First its own rows in that column, wherever they lie (G2): those above it, its room, go; those under it go when it was deleted or carried away, and stay when it was narrowed or pressed off (G8, `GAP.9`), as rows of the stack they lie in, its `u`'s (unowned under the top; in the stack whose `u` it was, the `u` that stack takes now). A pass frees nothing, whatever the switches, and is made after every other departure of the RELINK, every pass measured on the same canonical rows, taken then, so that a block passing in several columns keeps the same place whichever is taken first: its rows above it and under it stay, and its place stays as rows, its canonical body and the slack above it (the rows by which its stack did not hold it, the smaller of its canonical and its displayed slack: another block's temporary rows that the slack took on the screen do not become rows that push its neighbour), an anchor's as a narrowing with `GAP.9` on keeps them and a standing block's in place of its lend, which may be shorter than its body (it grew, G7); so the blocks under its place go down in neither measure, and rule 5 brings a block the steps moved to its target (step 1b, proposed). The carried block of a swap keeps there its temporary rows too, as rows, so they do not move on the screen either until the neighbour's step uses them or the carried block, coming back, takes them out (rule 1, O-21). Then its place. A standing block gives its lend back between the segs round it, whatever made it leave (S8), so a block that its growth had pushed comes back (H1). An anchor: its stack and the stack under it (whose `u` it is) join into one, which keeps the blocks standing in both; the block under it takes its `u`; narrowed or pressed off with `GAP.9` on, rows for its canonical height in `base` and its canonical slack there (the rows by which its stack did not hold it with no temporary height) stay too, as rows of the joined stack's `u` (unowned under the top), so the block under it does not move in the canonical layout. On the screen, a temporary height that reached the block under it only through this one stops pushing it, and one above it that the slack took now pushes it. An anchor with nothing below it in the column: the lowest block standing in its stack becomes the anchor of that stack, the others stand on in it; that block's lend and the rows under it go (GAP.3), and its room becomes rows of the stack's `u` (unowned under the top), as if it had come in there fresh (rule 4) [G2, C3, S8, G8]. (`leave`)
  3. **stand** (a block came into the column and something is below it: put there, created, widened, or pushed there). It stands in the stack that holds the nearest block below it already in the chain (blocks that come in at once are taken from the top in each of the two passes, so a lower one of the same pass is not yet there), in the seg just above that block. Its place there: a block carried or created there (one `base` does not hold, carried or not), or a block of a swap coming back, at the target, measured on the screen from the start of that seg, as the user aims at what is shown; a block of a swap that was an anchor in that column comes back there as an anchor, cutting the stack and the rows above and under it keeping their owners, so that taking it away later closes its place as in a document (G2) and each block keeps its own gaps (G4) (the owner, 2026-11-10, G11). Under a block moved by hand (the carried block, a block of a swap coming back) the rows there are those that keep the block under it at its canonical row, measured from the block's canonical row, the largest its columns give, on the canonical rows with rule 5 made (the moved blocks at their targets): where another block's temporary rows push it on the screen in that column while another column holds it, those rows are not written under it (§2.1) and the rest of the seg goes. A created block keeps under it the rest of the seg, so that taking it away gives every row back (INV-7 b); a block that came in by itself (widened, pressed aside) at its reference, measured against the canonical rows of M (above), so that no push of a temporary height is written (§2.1), and so is a block an undo puts back at its old cell, in every one of its columns, those it keeps included: it leaves them all and comes back into each (LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN §6.4); when its canonical row is above the end of the rows before it there (a temporary height carried it past a block of that column on the screen), it stands right under them, the order on the screen winning. The seg is cut at that place; the rows its body covers by its canonical height become its lend (G7: at most its height); the rows above it and under it stay where they are with their owners; put lower than the seg reaches, the rows between are its own, its room. In the free room above a head block, the rows are unowned and so is the lend. Several blocks that come in at once stand one after another, from the top, in the same stack: they are one path, and taking them away in any order gives the gap back whole (rule 2) [G4, C3, S8, G7]. (`enter`)
  4. **fresh** (a block came into the column and nothing is below it). It anchors a new stack: the rows from the anchor above it (or the top) to it, owned by that anchor (unowned under the top), counted as rule 3 measures a place [G1]. (`enter`)
  5. **fit**, last, and then FOLLOW in the displayed measure (RELINK also makes it on a copy of W before the blocks that come in by themselves, to measure them, M above, and writes nothing of that copy), whose rows are the result and the preview (INV-8). The rows SETTLE gave its blocks are targets, not constraints, visited in reading order (INV-5): a block of the steps, or the carried block (not an undo's, placed by its canonical row), that FOLLOW over the new stacks puts higher than its target gets, in its binding stack (the leftmost, when several bind it at the same row), the rows it lacks, owned by that stack's `u` (unowned under the top). No stack is ever cut: a block that FOLLOW puts lower than SETTLE had it, because a block that came in by itself at its canonical row pushes it on the screen (rule 3, §2.1), stands there. (In the model every row comes from its operations, and its `fit` with the rows SETTLE had changes nothing after such an entry.)
  (`relink`)
- **GAP.6 A block taken away.** Deleted, or carried away (DRAG.B1): rule 2 in every column it covered, then FOLLOW: the blocks under it rise into its place by its height and its own rows, wherever those lie in its columns; unowned rows and rows of others stay. A standing block taken away gives its lend back, so the block under it stays where the gap holds it [G2, C3]. (`remove`)
- **GAP.7 Gaps live in their columns.** A block carried to other columns never brings its rows along: rule 2 in the columns it left, rule 3 or 4 in the ones it came into, so gaps never add up [G4].
- **GAP.8 The first block of a column moved up.** By hand or pushed (S7), in a column where it is the first block, anchor or standing: the free room it leaves above the block under it, and the slack it used there, become its own rows (rule 1), so deleted or carried away it takes them along and the block under it rises by them. Moved down, it uses them up first [G6].
- **GAP.9 Narrowing is not deleting.** A block that stops covering a column because it got narrower or was pressed aside leaves the rows it owned under it there, as rows of the stacks they lie in. An anchor also leaves rows for its canonical height and its canonical slack there in the joined stack, so the block under it stays where it is in the canonical layout; on the screen a temporary height above it may now push it, or stop pushing it, until it ends (rule 2). A standing block gives its lend back as when it is deleted (S8): the block under it goes back to where the gap holds it, which is where it is unless the block's growth had pushed it. Switched off, its rows there go and an anchor's body leaves none, as after a deletion, and the block under it rises (rule 2) [G8].
- **GAP.10 A uniform shift.** When every block of a container moves by the same columns, an area's children kept on their sheet cells by its left side handle (§5.6), `shift` moves every stack with its columns, and the container's columns; nothing leaves or comes into a column, so every owner and lend stays. The wall part of `areaSide` calls it in phase 1a, before `base` is made, so `base` holds the shifted stacks. It checks no containment: carried inwards, the handle shifts the children by its move, and those that pass column 0 keep their stacks in the columns left of it, a state that exists only inside this SETTLE. The wall presses them back in phase 4 (P-SINK in phase 6 moves inside one it could not press); RELINK in phase 8 sees each leave the columns left of 0 as pressed off (rule 2) and come into its new ones by itself, at its canonical row (rule 3); phase 10 checks INV-4 once, after the wall, and a child still outside, one that cannot fit even alone, refuses the handle (AREA.3). (`shift`)

## 5. Module SETTLE: the one solver

`settle(start, change, memory, ctx) → {layout, start, memory, pressed, moves, grew}`, or `{refused}`, or `{failed}` (§5.5). SETTLE works on one container; `settleTree` (§5.6) drives it over a note's containers. Inputs: the layout a gesture or edit starts from (`start`: the displayed layout, §2.1), what the user changed (`change`, one of the change kinds of §5.2), the gesture's memory (§6.D, RESIZE.1, AREA.6), and `ctx`: the rules that are on (§1.6), the settings (§1.5), the passports of the kinds and the measure. Outputs: a layout satisfying INV-1 to INV-5, with its stacks found again; `start` and the memory as the letting-through wrapper left them (the caller keeps them for the next move); `pressed`, the block the wrapper would choose, which DRAG uses for the hold timer and VIEW lights; `moves`, the edges the change and the primitives moved (GAP.5); and `grew`, the change of an area's shown height between `start` and the result, in rows, negative when it shrank (§5.4, §5.6). SETTLE changes nothing else.

### 5.1 The phases, in this order

The pipeline is phases 1–10, run in this order every time; phase 3 acts only for a carry with `THROUGH` on. Phase 11 and the runs again after a choice or a swap are the letting-through wrapper (§5.1b); nothing inside the pipeline starts it again. Each phase reads only what the phases before it produced, visits several blocks in reading order of the layout it starts from, and adds the edges it moves to `moves`; with the columns each block left and came into and why, the swap and its passes, they make the transition the next RELINK reads (GAP's inputs; step 1b, proposed). Which phases a change runs is its kind's (§5.2); a phase whose rule is off (§1.6) is skipped, or replaced by the fallback that §1.6 names.

1. **Apply.**
   1a. Set the sizes the change sets (typing, a fold, a handle's `cw`, `ch` or `fold`, the hidden-lines count, a re-measure), through `resize` (GAP.4); FOLLOW; recompute the rest widths only of the elastic blocks whose content changed, on the rows FOLLOW gave (SIZE.1); FOLLOW again if a height changed. A change of room (a set width, a wall, the moved block, a block taken away) is met in phases 2–7 and settled in phase 9, never here.
   1b. Take away the block the change deletes, or the carried block when it is away (GAP.6, which ends with FOLLOW).
   The layout now is `base`: every RELINK of this SETTLE compares with it, so a column a carried block left is already closed when the block comes back (GAP.5, INV-7 b).
   1c. Put the moved block of the change (§5.2) at its target, with its target width, clamped to its container by its kind's rule (§5.2: DRAG.A3 for a carry, an arrow and a new block, a created block measured at the clamped width), so that every judge and primitive sees the geometry that is stored; it takes part in no stack until phase 8. When the memory pins it (§5.1b), phase 3 overrides its row.
   A change with no moved block goes on by its kind (§5.2): a wall at phase 2, an area's top or bottom handle at P-SQUEEZE, any other at phase 8.
2. **Sides.** The change kind's judge (§5.2) judges once every block that shares a column with the moved block: above or below it; and every block that hits it: met from the side or not. For a carry the judge is DRAG.B2 and B3 (DRAG owns them; the carry's kind carries them); for a wall it is the wall's (§5.3): every block sticking out past it is met from the side, and nothing is above or below it; for the width handle, whose moving edge is a wall pressing outward (§5.3), every block whose card the widened card now overlaps is met from the side; the other kinds judge nothing. A block gets both judgements; the side judgement wins in phase 4 and in the wrapper's choice, and «above or below» is used for a block met from the side only when P-ASIDE puts it back. A neighbour letting the block through is judged like any other block, but its judgements count only if the wrapper releases it; while it is in the memory it is judged neither way (PR-4). The judgements hold for the rest of this SETTLE; the sides met are returned with the memory for the next move (DRAG.B3), the phase itself writes nothing there.
3. **Letting through.** For a carry with `THROUGH` on: the pin and the swap of §5.1b, after the judgements of phase 2.
4. **Press aside.** Every block met from the side is pressed by P-ASIDE. A block that narrows changes its shown height: FOLLOW at once, so the blocks under it move with their gaps.
5. **Push up.** P-UP.
6. **Push down.** P-DOWN; then, for a change kind that sinks what has no room aside, P-SINK.
7. **Separate.** P-SEP.
8. **Relink.** RELINK against `base`, with the moved block as the carried block and the `moves` of phases 1c–7, which it uses up.
9. **Rest.** One fixed sequence, run once: (a) `RESTPASSES` passes of SIZE.8: a pass visits the elastic blocks in reading order of the layout it starts from, gives each its rest width on the rows it stands on and its height at that width (through `resize`; the RELINK of (c) cuts the lends of a text that ends shorter, GAP.4), and ends with FOLLOW; (b) the hits this leaves, one at a time, the first in reading order: P-REST, or P-SEP where P-REST does not apply; (c) RELINK against the layout after phase 8, with the moves of step (b) only (`by: 'rest'`). Step (a) may move blocks up (a text widened and got shorter); it runs a fixed number of passes, so it ends. Step (b) moves blocks only right within the container, or down, and only narrows elastic blocks, so their heights only grow: every step raises the sum of all the blocks' columns and rows, which has a ceiling (the container's columns, and the rows that the blocks' heights at their narrowest widths can fill below the lowest one); so (b) ends. `RESTSTEPS` (§1.5) guards it without measuring anything; if (b) ever takes more steps, which is a bug, SETTLE fails (§5.5). A text that (b) left narrower than its rest width gets it at the next SETTLE (E-2).
10. **Contain.** Check INV-4 by the change kind's rule (§5.2), moving nothing (the width was clamped in phase 1c, after which no phase leaves the container's columns; under a wall, the children `shift` put past column 0 were pressed back in phase 4 or moved inside in phase 6, and one still outside refuses the change, GAP.10): at the bottom, the change is refused (§5.4), or an area's content that passes its bottom grows the area, which SETTLE reports as `grew` (§5.6).
11. **Choose.** The wrapper (§5.1b), after the pipeline.

### 5.1b Letting through (`THROUGH`)

For a carry only: phase 3 inside the pipeline, and phase 11 with the runs again around it. It owns the neighbour letting the block through (DRAG.B4) and its row in the memory. With `THROUGH` off, phase 3 is skipped and the wrapper does nothing: no neighbour ever lets the block through, and a pushed neighbour is pushed as far as the block is carried [M2].

- **Phase 3**, in every run of the pipeline, after 1c and 2, if the memory holds a neighbour letting the block through, with the side it stands on, set when it was chosen: «going down» below means that it stands below the moved block, «going up» that it stands above. The pin and the swap add the edges they move to `moves` (`by: 'swap'`).
   - it leaves the memory when the target row lies past the row the moved block is pinned at, on the side away from the neighbour: before a swap the block was carried back, and the neighbour is pushed again like any other; after a swap the block goes on, and the neighbour stays where it is; either way it is an ordinary block with its phase 2 judgements for the rest of this SETTLE;
   - otherwise it is pinned where it stopped, the blocks under it following with their gaps as in P-DOWN (going up, as in P-UP), and the moved block is pinned right against it, on its touching row; `frow` drives only the swap test;
   - when `frow` puts the moved block's leading edge past the neighbour's middle, they swap: going down, the neighbour takes the moved block's pinned row and the moved block stands right under it; going up, the moved block takes the neighbour's top row and the neighbour stands right under it. The swap ends this run of the pipeline: the swapped layout is relinked, with this SETTLE's `base`, in a transition that declares the swap, its two blocks, the steps of 1c, the pin and the swap (the neighbour moving alone), the carried block passing the neighbour, and the neighbour passing a block of a column of its own the carried block does not cover (GAP's inputs), which it uses up as every RELINK does, and becomes `start` for B1 and for the run again, whose phase 1 makes a new `base` from it; the memory's neighbour becomes the swapped one with its new row, and the side it stands on, kept with it in the memory, flips; the pipeline then runs again on the swapped layout, at most once for every neighbour passed.
- **Phase 11**, after the pipeline, if the memory holds no neighbour letting the block through: the candidates are the blocks that, in this result, touch the moved block's leading edge in the memory's direction `dir` (§6.D), share a column with it, and are not met from the side; `pressed` is the first in reading order, or none. When the change says so, because the input's timer ran out (the pointer still within `STILL` of the hold point for `HOLD` against this same `pressed`) or Shift is held (DRAG.B4), `pressed` goes into the memory with the side it stands on (below when `dir` is down, above when it is up), and the pipeline runs once more from `start`, with that neighbour pinned [M6].

### 5.3 The primitives

- **Obstacle.** What presses and is never pushed: the moving edge of an area's side handle or of the width handle (a **wall**), the edges of a container, and a block that cannot move that way (`canMove`, §1.3). An obstacle is judged nothing, reaches nothing, anchors no stack and stands in none, and has no `pressed`. The right wall presses, in phase 4, as `p` of the first block of a chain, every child whose card passes the last column, until its card ends at that column; the left wall every child whose handle cell is left of column 0, until it is at column 0. The width handle's wall is the widened block's moving edge: every block its card now overlaps is met from the side and pressed away from it, to the right by a right edge, to the left by an area's left edge.
- **P-ASIDE.** A block `n` met from the side by a block `p` moves away from `p` so that their cards no longer share a column; `n` may stand with its handle cell on the last cell of `p`'s card. The blocks `n` then hits go aside the same way, a chain, in reading order. An elastic block gives way by SIZE.2. A block in the chain that cannot make all the room asked of it goes as far as it can (a set width a cell at a time, without pushing back the block pressing it; an elastic one down to its minimum), and the block pressing it then narrows by what is left, if it is elastic and above its minimum; if it cannot, the shortfall goes up the chain the same way. The moved block of the change never narrows here. If the first block of the chain cannot get out of the way, the whole chain is put back where this phase found it, and that block is handled by its change kind's «no room aside» (§5.2): for a carry, P-UP or P-DOWN by its side; for a handle, P-SINK. That is phase 4's only: called by P-REST in phase 9 (b), the chain is put back and P-REST's own rule applies, the pressing elastic block going down under the neighbour. A block that cannot move that way (`canMove`, §1.3) and the container's edges are obstacles in the chain [M5, W3]. (`shove`, `far`)
- **sink**, one primitive: a block goes down so that its top meets the lowest bottom of the blocks it is sunk against that it reaches or hits, repeated in reading order until it hits none of them; its lower neighbours follow with their gaps; asked to, it first moves to the nearest column where its card lies inside the container (in the transition, a leave and an entry as pressed aside: `press`, `self`). It has three uses, named for the rules that cite them:
  - **P-DOWN** (phase 6): every block judged below the moved block that reaches it sinks against the moved block. No limit but §5.4 [M2]. (`down`)
  - **P-SINK** (phase 6, for a change kind that sinks): a block P-ASIDE put back sinks against the blocks it hits; under a wall it first moves inside the container, under the width handle it keeps its column. An elastic block sinks at `min(natural width, TEXTW)` clamped to the container, and gets its rest width in phase 9 [W5]. (`squeezeH`)
  - **P-SEP** (phase 7, and phase 9 (b)): of two blocks that still hit, one sinks against the other. The moved block never moves. In phase 7, against the moved block the other goes by its side: judged below, it sinks; judged above, it goes up by P-UP. Everywhere else, and always in phase 9 (b), the lower one sinks, or on the same row the right one, or on the same row and column the later in reading order, as today's app separates blocks, so phase 9 (b) moves blocks only down [S6].
- **P-UP**, the mirror of `sink`. Every block judged above the moved block that reaches it goes up so that its bottom meets the moved block's top: first the gap above it shrinks, then the blocks above it go up too, each as high as it can, up to row 0. The moved block stands under the lowest bottom they could reach [M3]. (`up`, `topmost`)
- **P-REST.** At rest (phase 9), an elastic block at its minimum that still hits a neighbour on its right presses that neighbour aside by P-ASIDE, by the cells it lacks, the chain included, as a text growing in height pushes the blocks under it down (H1). When the neighbour cannot make the room, the elastic block goes down itself, under that neighbour. «Never pushes by itself» (W3) is about its widening beyond its minimum. Any other hit is separated by P-SEP [S5].
- **P-SQUEEZE.** An area's top or bottom handle carried inwards by one row, the other edge staying: the children move whole, with their gaps, into the free rows; when there are none, the gaps between children close, the ones furthest from the moving edge first; when none are left, elastic texts hide empty lines (SIZE.7), again furthest from the edge first, the end lines of a text first; when nothing can give, the step is refused. No child changes its column or its order. A handle carried several rows runs one step per row [W5]. (`squeezeV`, `squeezeTB`)
- **Reaches.** A block judged below the moved block reaches it when its top is above the moved block's bottom; one judged above reaches it when its bottom is below the moved block's top; overlapping or not, so a pointer that jumps past a block in one move still pushes it.

### 5.4 Containment

- A carried block is clamped at the sides and the top of its container by DRAG.A3; P-UP stops at row 0.
- At the bottom of the sheet there is no limit but `MAXCELL` (§1.5): a change that would put a block's bottom past it is refused.
- At the bottom of an area, a change other than the area's own handle makes the area's shown height grow: the area's SETTLE ends with its children's layout and reports `grew`, and `settleTree` runs its container's SETTLE with a size change of the area (§5.6); the preview shows both [W5, AREA.2].
- An area's own handle never makes the area taller and never puts a child outside: a result that would do either is refused (§5.5). A refused handle, an area's or the width handle with P-SINK off, then stands at the nearest rectangle to the one asked for, searched from it back towards the gesture's start, that SETTLE accepts; nothing about earlier moves of the handle is used (PR-7) [W5].

### 5.5 Refusal and failure

`{refused}` is not a bug: the change kind's containment refuses the change (§5.4), or the fallback of a switched-off rule is a refusal (DRAG.B5, P-SINK, §1.6). Nothing is written; the caller keeps its last accepted result: a carry stays at its last accepted target, a handle at the nearest accepted rectangle (§5.4).

A refusal answers a request to place something: a gesture, an arrow, a handle, a creation, an undo. A change of content that has already happened (typing, a fold, a re-measure, a child that made its area grow) is never refused: the content is the user's. When SETTLE cannot place it, refused at `MAXCELL` or failed, the content is kept and saved as usual, and the note goes into the safe mode for its geometry (LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN §3.5). Every block is shown at its last accepted place and the changed one at its new size, overlapping if it must; placement is off, and no place or size is written until the note is opened again.

SETTLE has one failure result, `{failed}`, returned when a bound is exceeded: the wrapper's runs again within one move (§5.1b: once for every neighbour swapped in that move, once after a choice), or `RESTSTEPS` in phase 9; and when RELINK refuses a transition that does not match its geometry (GAP's inputs). `settleTree` fails when one of its SETTLEs fails. It is a bug, never the user's error. The caller keeps the layout it had before the change and writes nothing; a gesture ends as a cancel (DRAG.C3); the dev look (VIEW.7) reports the change and the layout it started from, so that the case becomes a test.

### 5.6 Containers: `settleTree`

`settleTree(root, change, memory, ctx)` drives SETTLE over the containers of a note, so that SETTLE stays the work of one container and areas could nest without a rewrite [N1]:

- It runs SETTLE on the change's container. While a result reports a `grew` other than 0, it runs SETTLE on that area's container with a size change of the area (phase 1a), up to the sheet; so the blocks under an area that shrank come back by the same path (INV-7 b). A `{refused}` or `{failed}` anywhere is the result of the whole.
- A block that goes into or out of an area (AREA.7, «В область…», «З області») is two changes in order: taken away from the container it leaves (GAP.6) and put into the one it enters as a new block, away from the start (DRAG.B1). These and ungrouping and drawing an area are composite changes: `settleTree` takes them apart, and only the kinds of §5.2 reach SETTLE. Each container keeps its own `base`, `start`, stacks and `moves`; a carry keeps a `start` for every container it has touched.
- An area's side handle is a composite change too, run at every move from the gesture's start (AREA.6). Its outer rectangle is clamped first, to the container's columns, so that both parts work with the same width. Then, in the area, the moving edge is a wall (§5.2): for the left edge every child's column is first shifted by the edge's move with its stacks (`shift`, GAP.10, in phase 1a), so that every child keeps its cell on the sheet (AREA.5), and then the wall presses those left of column 0, containment checked only after it; the area's new shown height comes out as `grew`. After that, in the area's container, the area is a block whose set width changes, at its new column for the left edge, with its new height in the same change (the width handle's kind, §5.2): its moving edge presses the blocks it now overlaps aside or sinks them (AREA.7a), even when its height did not change. The change is accepted only when both are; a refusal in either refuses the whole, and the handle stands where both last accepted (§5.4). Both containers are among those it ran in, for the write.
- Ungrouping an area is the area taken away and each child created on the sheet in reading order (LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN §6); drawing an area is a new block.
- The displayed layout is made in the same order, children first (§2.1).
- The result is the layout of every container it ran in, and what the caller writes is compared container by container.

## 6. Module DRAG: carrying a block

DRAG turns a pointer into a target and a change, which the action's one computation previews and, on a drop, commits (LAYOUT-ENGINE-PLAN §2: `ops.js` runs it, the bridge stores it). Inputs: the layout the carry started from, the pointer, the keys, the carry memory. Outputs: the target and the change.

### 6.A The target

- **DRAG.A1 The cell.** The block's top-left card cell follows the pointer with the offset it was grabbed at, rounded to the nearest cell; `frow` is the unrounded row.
- **DRAG.A2 Sticking.** While the pointer is within `STICK` of the column the block was taken from, and the carry since it was picked up has been more vertical than horizontal, the column is held at that column; the same for the row when the carry has been more horizontal. A held row is also `frow`. When the pointer comes back within `STICK`, the value is held again [M7].
- **DRAG.A3 The edges of the container.** The target is clamped to its container: the block's top at row 0 or lower; a block with a set width stops at the last column where it fits whole; at the left edge every block stops. Past the right edge an elastic block narrows instead, so that its left edge stays under the pointer, down to `min(natural width, MINC)`, and then stops; carried back, it widens again (`DRAG.A3.narrow`). `geometry.js` gives the range, `size.js` the minimum, and phase 1c applies both [W4, C1].
- **DRAG.A3b Outside.** The state is «outside» when the pointer itself leaves the sheet's box.
- **DRAG.A4 Home.** Exactly on the cell it was taken from at the start of the carry, the state is «home» [C1].
- **DRAG.A5 The carried block's width.** A carried block keeps the width it had when the carry started, narrowed only at the edges by DRAG.A3. In the result, phase 9 gives an elastic block its rest width at the target (SIZE.1): it widens where there is room and stays narrow beside a neighbour on its right, and its own widening never presses anything, as an elastic block never pushes by itself (W3); only what the block overlaps is pressed. The preview shows that width (DRAG.C1, INV-8). A block with a set width keeps it [S4].

### 6.B What DRAG asks of SETTLE

DRAG.B2, B3 and B5 make the carry's change kind (§5.2): DRAG owns them, and SETTLE runs them as the kind's judge in phase 2 and as its «no room aside». DRAG.B4 is the letting through of §5.1b, phase 3 inside the pipeline and phase 11 with the runs again, with the hold timer of the input, which sends the change that says the hold ran out or Shift is held. So a later gesture, a finger on a touch screen, brings its own kind and leaves SETTLE as it is.


- **DRAG.B1 In its columns, or away.** «Away» is carry memory. It is set when the carried block's card shares no column with its place in `start`, and cleared only when it shares both a column and a row with that place. While not away, its place is held: nothing rises under it [M1]. While away, its place is freed in phase 1b [G2]. `start` is the layout at the start of the carry, or at the last swap (phase 3); DRAG.A2 and A4 always use the start of the carry. A block just made (EDIT.4, VIEW.4) is away from the start.
- **DRAG.B2 Above or below.** A block that shares a column with the carried block and stood in its columns in `start` keeps the side it had there, unless the block is away; any other is below when its middle is strictly below `frow`, otherwise above [M1, G2].
- **DRAG.B3 Met from the side.** A block that hits the carried block is met from the side when the overlap across the columns is smaller than the overlap along the rows, or equal and the last move between two targets was sideways (vertical before any move). It is pressed to the side its centre lies on, to the left when the centres coincide. Once met from the side, it stays so for the rest of the carry while it shares a row with the target rows; the record is cleared at a swap [M5].
- **DRAG.B4 Letting through.** DRAG keeps the hold point and starts the hold timer on SETTLE's `pressed`, restarting it when `pressed` changes or the pointer leaves `STILL`; the neighbour is chosen after the pipeline (phase 11), and pinned, released and swapped inside it (phase 3), as §5.1b says. While it lets the block through, the ghost stands right against it and a drop stores the block there; the dragged block itself follows the pointer. After a swap, the swapped neighbour, now on the other side, lets the block back the same way. Carried back before the swap, the neighbour is pushed again [M2, M6, M5].
- **DRAG.B5 No room aside.** A block met from the side that P-ASIDE put back is pushed by P-UP or P-DOWN according to DRAG.B2 for this move; it stays met from the side, and comes back up, aside and as wide as before, as soon as the block is carried back [M5].

### 6.C The result

- **DRAG.C1 The preview.** During the carry, the layout shown is SETTLE's result for the current target; at «home» or «outside», the layout at the start of the carry, before any swap. The carried block shows its width and height at the target (INV-8).
- **DRAG.C2 Drop.** Letting go in the «move» state stores the result as one undo entry [C2].
- **DRAG.C3 Cancel.** Esc, the right mouse button, «home» and letting go outside the sheet store nothing [C1].
- **DRAG.C4 Arrows.** The arrows of position mode jump, as today: left to the left edge of the container, right to the last column where the block fits, up right under the nearest block above it in its columns, down right over the nearest block below, or under the lowest block when there is none. A jump is a carry to that cell with an empty memory, dropped at once, so what it meets is pushed by the rules of a carry. The arrows are for throwing a block to an edge, not for moving it a cell; a later release may jump several selected blocks at once, lining them up on a side of the sheet [W15]. On a computer position mode is entered from the block's menu («Виділити»); on touch it waits for its own stage (W13).

### 6.D The carry memory

Kept for one carry only and cleared at «home» and at the drop, in three parts by who reads them:

- **What SETTLE reads**, passed to it and returned by it (PR-7): `start` for every container the carry touched (B1, §5.6); «away» (B1); the last target (B3); the direction of the last vertical move, `dir`: down before any vertical move, and changed only when the target row changes, by the sign of that change, which SETTLE reads from the target's row against `lastTarget`'s before phase 2, so that a pointer held still, or moved only sideways, keeps it (phase 11); the blocks met from the side and their sides (B3), cleared at a swap; the neighbour letting the block through, the row it stopped at and the side it stands on, flipped at a swap (B4); the lowered bottom of each area it was carried in (AREA.7).
- **What DRAG.A reads**: the start place and the pointer at the start (A2, A4).
- **What the input and the view keep**: the hold point and its timer (B4) are the input's; the note's width frozen for the carry, so that the sheet is not rescaled under the pointer, is VIEW's.

Nothing else carries over from one pointer move to the next.

## 7. Module RESIZE: the handles of a block

- **RESIZE.1 The width handle.** Carrying the handle at the card's right edge sets `cw`, from three cells to the container's right edge. The blocks it reaches are pressed aside by P-ASIDE, an elastic one giving way by `ORDER`, one with no room going down by P-SINK. Every move of the handle is a SETTLE from the layout the gesture started from (its memory), so carrying the handle back retraces the way; letting go stores one undo entry [W8, W3, C2].
- **RESIZE.2 Back to elastic.** A double click on the width handle removes `cw` [W3].
- **RESIZE.3 The height handle.** Released above the content, it writes `ch` and clears `fold`; released below it, it writes `fold` and clears `ch`; a double click clears both. `ch` below the content is ignored [H1].
- **RESIZE.4 A long paste.** A block made by a paste longer than 14 lines starts with `fold` 12 (`PASTEFOLD`); nothing else folds by itself [H2].

## 8. Module AREA: areas and their edges

An area is a container inside the sheet: inside it, every rule of SIZE, GAP, SETTLE, DRAG, RESIZE and EDIT holds with the area's own columns and rows; the interface keeps one level of areas, the engine is written recursively [N1]. Inputs: the area's rectangle, its children's layout. Outputs: the area's new rectangle and its children's layout, through SETTLE.

- **AREA.1 Containment.** The children always stay inside the area (INV-4, §5.4). At its sides a carried child behaves as at the edges of the sheet (DRAG.A3) [W4].
- **AREA.2 Height.** An area is never shorter than its content, and grows when a change other than its own handle pushes a child past its bottom (§5.4); it may be set taller with its bottom handle [W5].
- **AREA.3 A side handle carried inwards.** The opposite edge stays. The moving edge is a wall: what sticks out past it is pressed inwards by P-ASIDE; a block left with no room on its rows goes down by P-SINK, under what stands in its way, shifted inwards as far as it needs, and takes its rest width in phase 9. The handle stops (§5.4) where a block cannot fit even alone (a set width wider than the area, or an elastic block's minimum), and where a block, or a block it pushes, would come out past the bottom edge. The left handle works as the right one, mirrored [W5]. (`squeezeH`)
- **AREA.4 The top or bottom handle carried inwards.** P-SQUEEZE, one row at a time, until it is refused, then the handle stops (§5.4). For the bottom handle this supersedes H5 (an area folding to a height like text) until folding comes as one function in the next release [W5, A3].
- **AREA.5 A handle carried outwards.** The children keep their places on the sheet; hidden empty lines come back while there is room, the ones nearest the moving edge first [W5]. (`unsqueeze`)
- **AREA.6 One gesture, one entry.** The handle's memory is the layout and the rectangle at the start of the gesture, nothing else; every move of the handle is a SETTLE from it, so carrying it back retraces the way; letting go stores the area and its children as one undo entry [W5].
- **AREA.7 Entering and leaving.** By the pointer: a carried block goes into an area when the pointer comes inside its frame, and out when the pointer leaves it [A1]. (Tried against «whole» and «only by command» in the mockup.) An area counts as it was when the carry started, its bottom lowered to one row under the lowest of its other blocks when they need it: the carried block may stand right under the lowest of them, pushing it down or after swapping with it, and stays in; carried lower, it goes out. Within one carry this bottom never moves up, so a swap that lifts the neighbour does not put the pointer outside; the carried block's own body never keeps it in. A set width wider than the area never goes in. The container the block leaves lays out without it, as if it were deleted (GAP.6); in the one it enters it is a new block, away from the start (DRAG.B1); an area never lets a block through by holding; and «В область» puts the selected block into the nearest area it fits, under its lowest block at the left edge, as the app does, while «З області» puts it in the area's container under the area, in the column it had inside [A1, A2].
- **AREA.7a An area on its container.** On the sheet an area stands as a block with a set width, with one free cell round its frame for its handles: what stands beside it, above it or below it keeps that cell free (its extent, §1.1), and it is pushed and followed by the same rules as a block. A block carried on the sheet over an area it does not go into stops at the frame, on the side it came from (where it last stood clear of the area, or, just out of another container, where the pointer is); pressed against the left side, an elastic block narrows first, as at the sheet's edge (DRAG.A3). A side handle carried outwards presses what it reaches aside, and what has no room goes down under the area (P-SINK); the blocks under the area follow its bottom handle both ways [A2].
- **AREA.7b Drawing an area (as in the app).** A drag on the empty sheet, outside every area, draws an empty area from the cell pressed to the cell under the pointer, at least 4 × 2 cells; it is put in as a new block is (EDIT.4), so what it covers makes room by the same rules, above or below judged by its frame; the preview shows where it will stand. Areas are never nested. In the reading mode and in the app look, an area's handles show only under the pointer, as a block's handle does (VIEW.6a) [A2].
- **AREA.8 The sheet's right edge.** The sheet is a container too, and its right edge is a handle. Carried outwards into the white room of a wider window, it widens `SHEETW`, up to the edge of the screen and no further; nothing moves. Carried inwards, it narrows `SHEETW` as an area's side handle narrows an area (AREA.3), except that the sheet has no bottom edge to stop at. Letting go stores it with one undo entry [W7].
- **AREA.9 Long content in an area.** Decided (A3, 2026-11-04): the first release folds nothing new; an area with no set height follows its blocks, and its existing collapse to the header stays. The next release makes folding one function with one standard stored field for every kind that folds (text, area, code, to-do lists) and settles the ways tried in the mockup: Two needs, «I see the blocks, only shorter» and «I know they are there, I need not see them», and these ways, all heights of SIZE.6 and none stored as a layout:
  1. A long text folds itself (H1): from its menu, «Згорнути до 5 рядків», or with its height handle; the area follows its blocks, so it gets smaller. An area's height is its content's, or its set height `ch` from its bottom handle when larger; growth with the content is not kept once the content shrinks (AREA.2).
  2. An area has three states, switched on its header: all blocks as they are; «Рядки», every block one first line with «+N рядків», a click opens that one for reading (a temporary height, H3); collapsed, a strip with «N блоків», whose hover or tap lists the first lines, and a click on one opens the area on that block. A compact or collapsed area takes no carried block, makes no new block on a click, and hides its handles. The state is stored with the area.
  3. A variant of the top and bottom handles: when P-SQUEEZE can give nothing more, the handle folds the long texts, the one furthest from the moving edge first, a row at a time, never below 3 rows shown, instead of stopping; the folds are stored on release (W5 says the handle stops; this is offered as an alternative).
  Every kind that folds will fold the same way: one stored field, its passport reading it, one «Розгорнути» row and one menu command (A3).

## 9. Module EDIT: clicks, the caret and new blocks

Inputs: a click or a hover on the sheet, the blocks' rectangles. Outputs: where the caret goes, or a change for SETTLE.

- **EDIT.1 What a point belongs to.** A point belongs to a block when it lies on its card, the spare room of a set size included, or within `NEAR` to the right of the card or under it. A point on a card belongs to that card even when it is within `NEAR` of another; a point within `NEAR` of two cards belongs to the first in reading order. Any other point of a container is empty sheet [M8]. A point in the white room beyond `SHEETW` is no part of the sheet: a click there does nothing [W7].
- **EDIT.2 Before the click.** Over a block, its whole frame shows and the cursor is a text cursor; over the empty sheet, the cursor is a cell and nothing else shows [M8].
- **EDIT.3 A click on a block.** On its text, the caret goes there. To the right of a line, it goes to the end of that line. Below the text, in the spare room or within `NEAR` under the card, it goes to the first empty line after the text, made if there is none: a provisional line, a temporary height (§1.2), which becomes text only when something is written on it and is taken back when the caret leaves without it. Clicking below again finds the same line; only Enter adds lines. A block with spare room keeps its height; one without grows by a row, as with Enter [M8].
- **EDIT.4 A click on the empty sheet.** A single click starts a new empty elastic text there, its card on the clicked cell: a change for SETTLE (§5.2). Left empty, it is removed as a delete (GAP.6); if nothing else has changed since the click, the layout before the click is restored instead, and the click and the removal together make no undo entry [M8, C3].
- **EDIT.5 The caret brings hidden lines back.** Putting the caret in a block that hides empty lines sets its count to zero: a change of §5.2 that writes [W5].
- **EDIT.4a A new block carried before anything is written in it.** It keeps the caret while it is carried, so it is not taken away under the hand, and the caret is back in it after the drop. Left empty afterwards, it goes away with its carries too: the layout is the one before the click (EDIT.4) [M8, C3]. The removal waits until no gesture holds the block (a block being carried or given the caret back stays), and it takes away only that block and restores the others' places: nothing else on the sheet is rebuilt.
- **EDIT.6 EDIT's memory.** The block and the line of a provisional line, and the text before it, until the caret leaves; the block made by EDIT.4, whether anything was written in it and the layout before the click, until the caret leaves it.

## 10. Module VIEW: windows, scale, the narrow view, the look

Inputs: the stored layout, the window width, the settings, and during a gesture the note's width frozen in its memory. Outputs: what is drawn. VIEW never writes the layout (INV-6), and nothing it hides or shows changes how anything is measured (PR-8).

- **VIEW.1 Window and scale.** The width that counts is the sheet's, `SHEETW`. The visible area is the window less the list of notes when the list is docked. A visible area wider than the sheet shows the sheet with its dot grid and plain white room to its right, where nothing can be placed. The scale is fitted to the width the note uses, up to its rightmost block, not to the whole sheet [W12]: a visible area at least that wide shows the note at full size, and the rest of the sheet, when the window is narrower than `SHEETW`, is reached by the sideways scroll and the hand; a narrower one shows the note scaled down to fit its used width, no smaller than `MINSCALE`; narrower still, the narrow view (VIEW.2). The user can zoom in from the fitted scale, and from the narrow view to the real layout; a horizontal scrollbar appears and the hand works (the scale is a setting of the device, never stored in the note). The first release places blocks on a computer only [W13]: a device with a mouse or a trackpad, at `MINSCALE` or more, edits the layout. A phone or a tablet reads, writes, deletes and creates blocks as VIEW.3 allows and moves nothing, whatever its width: at `MINSCALE` or more it shows the real layout so, below it the narrow view, with «Показати як на компʼютері» only to look at (VIEW.5, W6). How touch devices move blocks waits for their own stage (O-5). Widened again, the note is as it was. During a gesture the visible area and the scale are frozen [W2, W7]. The fitted scale is recomputed when a note opens and after an action (a drop, a handle, a creation, a deletion); typing never changes it while the caret is in the block, and it is recomputed when the caret leaves [W16].
- **VIEW.1a The list of notes.** Hiding it only widens the visible area; it never changes a note. Opened, it pushes the note aside when the note still fits beside it at its current scale, and otherwise opens over the note like a drawer, so that opening it never rescales the note [W7].
- **VIEW.1c A note without a width.** A note begun on a phone has no `SHEETW` yet: on a device that is not a computer it fits the screen, like a web page. The first time a computer opens it, the width that computer shows with the list of notes open becomes its `SHEETW`, stored and synced; from then on it is a note like any other. The blocks made on the phone keep their places, a column at the left margin (W6) [W7].
- **VIEW.1b The hand.** In the hand mode a drag moves the sheet in any direction and does nothing else: no click, no carry, no new block. It is on while Space or the middle mouse button is held, or while the hand button is pressed, a toggle next to the scale [S2]; two fingers on a touchpad or a touch screen scroll the sheet as always [W7].
- **VIEW.2 The narrow view.** The blocks one under another in reading order, an empty row between them, none wider than the screen; an area as a frame with its children inside in the same way; long blocks folded at `FOLDPHONE` rows, a temporary height. The stored layout under it keeps `SHEETW`: nothing measured for the narrow view feeds back into it [W2, W6, H6].
- **VIEW.3 What the narrow view allows.** Reading, writing and formatting text, following links, copying, unfolding and folding for reading, deleting and creating blocks. Not carrying, the arrows, the handles, «В область…» and «З області» [W6].
- **VIEW.4 A block made on a narrow screen.** Made after block X, it goes under X on the computer, in X's column, one empty row under X's bottom, as a change of §5.2 like EDIT.4. «+» puts one under every block at the left margin. In the narrow view it shows by reading order, so it can appear after a block that stands beside X [W6].
- **VIEW.5 «Показати як на компʼютері».** The computer layout scaled to the screen, only to look at [W6].
- **VIEW.6 The look during a gesture.** Every block shows its real size as a thin frame, its empty part included; the carried block gets a background; a selected block shows its frame only. Guides follow the carried block: a hairline along the left edge of its card and one along its top, across the sheet, solid where they meet the starting place or the left or top edge of another block, the row one paler than the column. A neighbour pressed for `HOLD` lights its outline while the time runs and keeps it lit while it lets the block through. The width handle shows only under the pointer [V1, M6, M7].
- **VIEW.6a The note's view settings.** Where the text stands and which field (P18) are a setting of the app, synced, which a note may override with its own, stored and synced (in the first release a setting of the device, every variant offered and today's look the default, for the owner to try on real notes; P18); changing either is a re-measure, never a user action on the note: the wide field row is a temporary height (§1.2), and the text placement changes what texts measure, as a font does (SIZE); neither has an undo entry, and what the re-measure moves is written with the next user action in that container (INV-6). A note's view switch chooses between borders always visible and the reading mode: at rest only text on a clean sheet; the borders of all blocks show while a block is carried (VIEW.6), and while the pointer is held still on the empty sheet for a moment (they go when it is let go, and nothing is created). A new block starts with no frame, only the caret, and while it is started nothing else shows: no borders of the other blocks (O-17). In the reading mode the block's handle shows only under the pointer, while the caret is in the block and while it is carried; on a touch screen, with no pointer to hover with, it stays faint. With borders always visible the handle is always shown [S1]. Borders show as a thin frame or with a background, chosen in the same view settings. By default a note is in the reading mode with a thin frame; the note's menu switches to borders always visible and to a background [V2].
- **VIEW.7 Two looks.** The app look shows only what VIEW.6 lists. The testing look («dev») adds what the rules work with: the gap runs with their owners and sizes, the blocks' letters, sizes in cells, row numbers, the outline of the place a block was taken from, the focus ring. Both looks are kept in the app; the testing look is switched on, deep in the app's settings, to track down a bug [V1, V2]. A block selected with the keyboard shows its frame solid instead of dashed: that is the focus in the app look [V1].
- **VIEW.8 The rules list** [R1]. With the testing look, the settings show «Правила»: every switchable rule of §1.6 with a checkbox and a line on what it does. Turning one off turns off the rules that need it, shown greyed with the reason; turning it on again brings back what was on. It is a setting of the device, never stored in a note, every rule on by default. Changing it opens the note again under the new rules (as a change from outside), which writes nothing by itself; what it moves is written with the next user action in that container, as any re-measure's (INV-6). In the first release the list is on `.dev` only.
- **VIEW.9 The carried block at the edges.** The carried block as drawn stops at its container's edges as its ghost does (DRAG.A3): it never slides out of the sheet, so nothing scrolls past it [W4, C1]. When the window shows only part of the sheet, the sheet scrolls by itself while the pointer holds the carried block at the window's edge: sideways up to the sheet's own edges, and downwards with no limit, the sheet growing under the block as it does today (§5.4) [W16].

## 11. Precedence

The phases of §5.1 order everything within one change, and the table of §5.2 says what differs between changes. These rules settle the rest.

- **PR-1 Home and outside first.** At «home» or «outside» SETTLE does not run for that move (DRAG.A4, C1).
- **PR-2 One target per move.** Every phase uses the target of DRAG.A, never the raw pointer: a held row is the row for the sides and the swap too (DRAG.A2, B2, phase 3).
- **PR-3 Sides are sticky and exclusive.** A block met from the side is pressed aside, or pushed for lack of room; it is never a candidate to let the block through (DRAG.B3, B5, phase 11).
- **PR-4 The neighbour letting the block through is fixed.** While it is in the memory its phase 2 judgements do not count and it takes no part in phases 4–7; released in phase 3, it is an ordinary block with them.
- **PR-5 Containment by §5.4.**
- **PR-6 FOLLOW and RELINK only at their points.** FOLLOW runs in phase 1a, at the end of GAP.6, inside phase 3 for a pinned neighbour's lower blocks, inside phase 4 after a narrowing, in phase 9, after every RELINK (GAP.5 rule 5), and in making the displayed and the canonical layout (§2.1); RELINK in phase 3 after a swap, phase 8 and phase 9; `cap` in every RELINK, after rule 2 (GAP.4), and `shift` in phase 1a of an area's left side handle. An area that grows hands its new height to its container's own SETTLE (§5.4), which runs these steps there. No phase reads gaps written in the middle of another.
- **PR-7 No hidden memory.** SETTLE reads nothing but `start`, `change` and the gesture's memory, and writes nothing but its outputs; the caller keeps the returned `start` and memory for the next move.
- **PR-8 Presentation never changes measurement.** Nothing VIEW draws or hides changes a width or a height.

## 12. Lessons from the mockup's bugs

Each bug found while the owner tried the mockup came from two rules meeting without a stated order. The rules above are cut so that each has one place where it is decided.

1. **A «home» zone of one cell swallowed pushes** next to the starting place (2026-11-01). Home is the exact cell only (DRAG.A4, PR-1).
2. **Sticking held the row, the side judgement used the pointer**, so neighbours above were pushed down (2026-11-01). One target per move (PR-2).
3. **A neighbour pushed down for lack of room, then held still over, let the block through** and stayed down (2026-10-30). Sides are exclusive (PR-3).
4. **A block brought back into its columns lower down pushed its old lower neighbour through itself** (2026-10-31). Away and back (DRAG.B1, B2).
5. **An area's height did not follow children that went down**, and they stuck out (2026-10-31). Containment in one place (§5.4).
6. **A carried block's gap piled onto the gaps of its new column** (2026-10-27). Gaps live in their columns (GAP.7).
7. **Room reserved for a testing label made texts wrap early**, which looked like free room in the app look (2026-11-01). Presentation never changes measurement (PR-8).
8. **A new block on the same row as a neighbour judged that neighbour above it** (2026-10-31). A new block is away from the start (DRAG.B1).
9. **The narrow view measured elastic widths with the phone's columns** (2026-10-31). The layout under the narrow view keeps `SHEETW` (VIEW.2).
10. **The bottom handle of an area closed a gap and reopened another** when it moved several rows at once (2026-10-31). One row at a time (P-SQUEEZE).
11. **A state class of the mockup collided with the class of another element** and narrowed the sheet, so clicks on its right part did nothing (2026-11-01). For the engine: the view's state names live in their own namespace, and every interface scenario clicks across the whole sheet.
12. **The width handle, the area's handles, a new block and the carry each had their own pushing code** in the mockup, with slightly different rules. One solver (§5).
13. **Found in review, not yet seen by the owner:** the mockup compares gaps with the start of the carry, so a block carried away and back loses a row of its upper neighbour's gap (D-11). RELINK compares with `base` (phase 1).
14. **The carried block slid past the edge of the sheet while its ghost stopped**, which made the sheet scroll; **a long press on the empty sheet started the browser's own text selection and drag** (2026-11-02). The drawn block obeys the same edges as the ghost (DRAG.A3), and the sheet selects nothing outside the texts.
15. **A new empty block grabbed by its handle lost its caret, was taken away under the hand, and the carry never ended**, so every click on the sheet was ignored afterwards (2026-11-02). A block being started keeps its caret while carried (EDIT.4a), and for the engine: a block is never removed under a gesture, any gesture whose block is gone ends at once (§6.D is cleared), and a new press ends any gesture still hanging before it does anything else, so the sheet can never stay in a carry.
16. **Pushing a neighbour down, the block let it through after one or two rows** (2026-11-04). The hold timer restarted only when the block changed cell, so a hand moving slowly, more than `HOLD` per row, ran it out while moving; and in an area the pointer was compared with the area's first bottom, so a block pushing a neighbour past it left the area. Then a swap inside an area threw the block out at once: the neighbour that let it through stood still while the pointer went down through it, past the area's bottom, and the swap lifted that bottom further. For the engine: the timer restarts whenever the pointer leaves `STILL`, as DRAG.B4 says, whatever the target does; an area keeps a carried block down to one row under its lowest other block, and that bottom never moves up within a carry (AREA.7).
17. **A text that widened by itself between two blocks took the upper one's gap as its own**, so deleting it lifted the lower block to the upper one (found by the independent review, 2026-11-04, and shown in the mockup's «Расширился между»). A block that comes between by itself counts as put into the gap (GAP.5 rule 3, S8).
18. **A gap that did not hold its lower block lost rows every time a text beside it grew and shrank back** (found while trying rule 3 in the mockup, 2026-11-04: in «Две колонки» C grown by four lines and back took E's gap of four rows from F, and deleting C then lifted F). A row that FOLLOW or a height changed is not a move (GAP.5 rule 1).

### 14.2 Where the mockup differs

- **D-1** One area per example, standing for the sheet; areas inside the sheet, entering and leaving, folding an area are not in it.
- **D-2** It measures text in the page; the engine asks a measuring function (E-3).
- **D-3** Its elastic maximum is 16 cells; the app's is 26 [W3].
- **D-4** Its sheet's columns are the window's when the window is wider than the note, so rest widths depend on the window; the engine uses `SHEETW` [W7].
- **D-5** It draws a fresh block's placeholder «Пишите…» and counts its width; the engine never measures a placeholder (§1.1).
- **D-6** Its rows are stored for every block, and its temporary heights move the blocks under them in the stored state; the engine derives every row from the stacks (§2.1).
- **D-7** Its width handle starts every move from the gesture's start, as RESIZE.1 says, but separates the blocks by its own rule (the block more to the left stays, the other moves aside or down); the engine follows P-ASIDE and P-SINK [W8].
- **D-8** Its carried elastic block keeps the width it had at rest, as DRAG.A5 says, but takes its rest width at the target, and an area's pushed-down child its width back, only after the release; the engine shows them in the preview (phase 9, INV-8).
- **D-9** Its choice of the neighbour that lets the block through depends on the order of storage, and the Shift path and the hold path choose differently; the engine follows phase 11.
- **D-10** Its separation after a size change keeps the block more to the left (or, in one column, the upper one) and moves the other aside or down, and an elastic block at its minimum pushes its neighbour; the engine follows P-SEP and P-REST.
- **D-11** Its RELINK compares with the start of the carry, so a block carried away and brought back touching its old upper neighbour loses that neighbour's row, and a second carry away does not give it back; the engine compares with `base` (phase 1).
- **D-12** It recomputes rows only at the end of a carry step, so a block that grows during SETTLE can close the gap under it; the engine runs FOLLOW where sizes change (PR-6).
- **D-13** With «never narrow» its text ignores the neighbours at rest and pushes them aside; the engine caps it by its room (SIZE.4, W9).
- **D-14** The mockup splits gaps over pairs of neighbours, as these rules did before the stacks (round 3 of the review): two gaps that converge under one inserted block keep only one tail, and a head block's row is a cache. The executable model `tests/lab/gap-model` is the reference for gaps, not the mockup.
